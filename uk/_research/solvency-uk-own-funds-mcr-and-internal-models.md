**What this stream owns**

- The **Own Funds Part** of the PRA Rulebook in full: basic and ancillary own funds; the
  **reconciliation reserve**; the Tier 1 unrestricted / Tier 1 restricted / Tier 2 / Tier 3
  classification and the features each tier requires; the participations deduction; the
  **ring-fenced-fund and matching-adjustment-portfolio adjustment to own funds** (Own Funds 3L);
  the **eligibility limits** for covering the SCR and the MCR; and the pre-issuance notification
  regime.
- The **Minimum Capital Requirement Part** in full: the MCR formula, the linear formula and its
  **long-term (life) factors**, the 25% / 45% corridor, the absolute floor, quarterly
  calculation, and the reporting duty when a corridor limit binds.
- The **Surplus Funds Part** and the **With-Profits Part**, and with them the single most
  load-bearing capital fact for the with-profits product: **a with-profits estate that qualifies
  as surplus funds is excluded from technical provisions and becomes unrestricted Tier 1 own
  funds, but sits inside a ring-fenced fund and is therefore restricted own funds for the
  reconciliation-reserve and SCR calculations.**
- The **Solvency Capital Requirement – Internal Models Part** in full: permission, partial
  models, safeguards, model change, reversion, non-compliance, the **use test**, statistical
  quality standards, calibration standards, the **deleted profit-and-loss-attribution chapter and
  the Analysis of Change chapter that replaced it**, validation, documentation, external models
  and data, and the five partial-internal-model integration techniques.
- The **Undertakings in Difficulty Part**: what happens on an SCR breach and on an MCR breach.
- The own-funds policy layer: SS2/15, SoP10/24, CP4/26 and PS18/26; and the internal-model
  policy layer: SS1/24 and SS15/16.

**Seven retrieval facts that change how this material must be documented**

1. **"Expected profits included in future premiums" (EPIFP) is dead in Solvency UK reporting and
   disclosure.** The **Own Funds Part contains no EPIFP rule at all** (searched in full; zero
   occurrences of "expected profit" in the retrieved Part) [R77]. PS3/24 records that eight
   respondents "welcomed the deletion of the expected profits included in future premiums (EPIFP)
   requirement in S.23.01" and that the PRA "amended the template instructions to clarify that
   the **EPIFP requirement is being removed from all reporting, including disclosure**"
   (PS3/24 ¶4.43–4.44) [R86]. The only retrieved live text of the definition is assimilated EU
   law: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/35 **Article 1(46)** on legislation.gov.uk,
   shown as up to date on 05 August 2026 (see Extracted mechanics §8 and Gaps). Any UK document that
   asserts an EPIFP own-funds disclosure is wrong.
2. **The Solvency II profit-and-loss attribution requirement no longer exists in the UK.**
   Internal Models **Chapter 13 "Profit and Loss Attribution" is `[Deleted]` in its entirety**
   (13.1, 13.2 and 13.3 all `[Deleted]`, date-stamped 31/12/2024) and is replaced by a new
   **Chapter 13A "Analysis of Change"** — a year-on-year comparison of the SCR with documented
   reasons, submitted to the PRA under Reporting 2 from the first financial year end on or after
   **31 December 2025** [R81]. A drafter must not carry the EU Art. 123 P&L-attribution
   description into UK text.
3. **The Own Funds Part carries two live eligibility chapters that are expressed on different
   bases.** Chapter 4 (rules dated **01/01/2016**, noting Art. 98 SII) states the limits as
   fractions of *eligible own funds*; Chapter 4A (rules dated **31/12/2024**) states them as
   percentages of the *SCR* and of the *MCR*. Both appear in the 05/08/2026 present view. They
   are not arithmetically the same test. Recorded as a conflict in "Gaps and caveats", not
   resolved here [R77].
4. **The MCR absolute floor is in sterling, not euro.** MCR 3.2(2): **£3,500,000** for firms with
   Part 4A permission for long-term insurance [R78]. UK documentation must not carry the EU euro
   figures.
5. **The Own Funds Part has a live future view after 31/12/2026.** Rules 3A.1, 3B.1, 3C.1, 3D.1,
   3E.1, 3F.1 and 3G.1 all carry a "Future version … after 31/12/2026" marker. PS18/26
   (29 July 2026) confirms that the rule change removing the **classification permission for
   equity-accounted subordinated liabilities** "will therefore take effect from **31 December
   2026**" (PS18/26 ¶3.19, ¶1.32) [R83d]. The future text itself was **not retrieved**. Every
   Own Funds rule citation downstream must carry the as-of date it was read (05/08/2026 here).
6. **`prarulebook.co.uk` and `bankofengland.co.uk` return HTTP 403 to plain fetchers.** Every
   Rulebook and Bank of England retrieval below was made with a browser User-Agent and returned
   HTTP 200. The PRA Rulebook **Glossary letter pages could not be retrieved for this session**
   (all URL forms tried returned 404 or 500 — see Gaps); definitions quoted below come from
   glossary pages retrieved earlier in the same effort (letters R and T) or from Part-specific
   definition chapters.
7. **The rulebook is a dated-view system.** All Part citations below are the **05/08/2026 present
   view** unless stated. Rule date-stamps are recorded because they identify which text is the
   post-restatement (31/12/2024, PS15/24 [R6]) version and which survives from 01/01/2016.

## Existing entries (R1–R38, and sibling-stream entries) that bear on this stream

- **[R1] PRA Rulebook — Technical Provisions Part.** TP 9.1(3) is the hinge: firms must take into
  account "all payments to policyholders, including future discretionary bonuses … **unless those
  payments fall within Surplus Funds 2.1**" (verified in the retrieved Part text). That exclusion
  is what turns a with-profits estate into own funds instead of a liability.
- **[R2] PRA Rulebook — Matching Adjustment Part.** An MA portfolio is *not* a ring-fenced fund
  (the RFF glossary definition expressly excludes it) but is subject to the **same** notional-SCR
  and no-diversification treatment under Own Funds 3L.1 and SCR-SF 9.1.
- **[R4] SI 2023/1346 (Risk Margin Regulations).** Fixes the risk-margin cost-of-capital rate at
  4%; relevant here only because the risk margin sits inside technical provisions and therefore
  *reduces* the excess of assets over liabilities that feeds basic own funds.
- **[R6] PS15/24 — Restatement of assimilated law.** The instrument that created the current
  Own Funds, MCR, Surplus Funds and Internal Models rule text (nearly every rule below is
  date-stamped 31/12/2024 as a result) and deleted SS15/15.
- **[R7] PS2/24 — Adapting to the UK insurance market.** The policy statement under which SS1/24
  (internal model expectations) was published.
- **[R9] FCA Handbook COBS 20 — With-profits.** The Surplus Funds Part repeatedly defers to "any
  relevant provisions of the FCA Handbook" for shareholder transfers, attributable liabilities and
  permitted charges (Surplus Funds 3.1(3)–(5), 3.3(8), 3.3(10), 3.4(6), 3.6); COBS 20 is also the
  test for whether a with-profits sub-fund is a separate with-profits fund and therefore a
  separate RFF (SS14/15 ¶2.3 [R80b]).
- **[R33]/[R34]/[R35] FRC TAS 100, TAS 200, IFoA APS L1.** The professional layer over internal
  model and surplus-fund work; cited, not re-created.
- **[R36] Proxy Modelling Working Party** and **[R37] Model Risk Working Party.** The practitioner
  literature behind the proxy-model / heavy-model split that an internal model forces on a
  liability cash flow engine (see the "Proxy / heavy-model split" row in Model hooks, and
  Extracted mechanics §14 on SCR-IM 10.10).
- **[R84] PRA Rulebook — Reporting Part.** Names the own funds templates **IR.23.01 – IR.23.04**
  (annual; **IR.23.01 also quarterly**, under Article 9(1)(k)) and the MCR templates **IR.28.01**
  (firms carrying on only long-term business, or only general insurance / reinsurance business)
  and **IR.28.02** (firms carrying on both). **IR.23.05 is a Lloyd's template** (the Society, SCR
  and MCR information, annual and quarterly) — verified, and recorded because the code looks like
  an own-funds code and is not. There is **no `IRR.23` template**: the per-ring-fenced-fund /
  per-MA-portfolio series that exists is **IRR.02.01** (balance sheet), **IRR.12.01 / IRR.12.05 /
  IRR.12.06** (life TPs, with-profits) and **IRR.25.04 / IRR.25.05** (SCR), so **the notional SCR

- **[R86] PS3/24 — Reporting and disclosure phase 2.** The source for the EPIFP deletion

- **[R87] PS15/25 / PS18/26.** PS18/26 is re-cited here as **[R83d]** for its own-funds content

- **[R95] SS19/16 — ORSA** and **[R97] SS17/16 — internal models: assessment, model change and

### B. The Minimum Capital Requirement, and what happens when it is breached

#### R78. PRA Rulebook — **Minimum Capital Requirement Part** (as at 05/08/2026)
- **Publisher:** Prudential Regulation Authority (Bank of England)
- **URL:** https://www.prarulebook.co.uk/pra-rules/minimum-capital-requirement/05-08-2026
- **Doc type:** rulebook part (dated as-at view). **Accessed:** 2026-08-06.
- **fetched_ok:** yes (16,664 chars; read in full — the Part is short)
- **Annotation:** Short, entirely formulaic, and fully transcribed in Extracted mechanics §9.
  Verified: the firm must hold **eligible own funds covering the MCR** (2.1, Art. 128 SII); the
  MCR is `max(MCR_combined, AMCR)` (3.1A) where `MCR_combined = min(max(MCR_linear, 0.25·SCR),
  0.45·SCR)` (3.1B); the absolute floor is **£3,500,000** for long-term insurance business
  (3.2(2)); and 3.3 independently restates the corridor as "neither below 25% nor above 45% of the
  firm's SCR … **including any capital add-on which has been imposed**". The linear component
  splits into a general-insurance and a long-term part (3A.1); the **long-term linear formula
  (3C.1)** has four technical-provision terms and a capital-at-risk term, with a **negative
  coefficient on future discretionary benefits**. Calculation is **at least quarterly** with
  reporting under Reporting 2.1–2.5 (4.1); where a corridor limit binds, the firm must give the
  PRA information allowing a proper understanding of why (4.2). Chapter 6 carries the non-life
  segment factor table (alpha_s, beta_s), transcribed only in outline here because it does not
  bite on life products. Old rule 3.1 is `[Deleted]`; 3.1A, 3.1B, 3.2, 3A, 3B, 3C and Chapter 6
  are date-stamped **31/12/2024**; 2.1, 3.3 and Chapter 5 (Lloyd's) survive from 01/01/2016; 4.1
  from 31/12/2021. Related guidance named on the Part page: **SS4/15 — Solvency II: the solvency
  and minimum capital requirements** (not retrieved).

## Extracted mechanics

Notation: plain text. `·` is multiplication, `^` exponentiation, `sqrt(x)` the square root.
Rule references are PRA Rulebook rules as at **05/08/2026** unless stated.

### 1. The own funds stack in one picture [R77]

```
Solvency UK balance sheet (Valuation Part [R111], TP Part [R1])
   assets at fair value
 - technical provisions (BEL + risk margin)          <- streams A/B
 - other liabilities
 = EXCESS OF ASSETS OVER LIABILITIES

BASIC OWN FUNDS (Own Funds 2.2)
 = (excess of assets over liabilities - own shares held by the firm)
 + subordinated liabilities

OWN FUNDS (Own Funds 2.1)
 = basic own funds + ancillary own funds (permission required, 2.5)

then classify every item into Tier 1 unrestricted / Tier 1 restricted / Tier 2 / Tier 3
(Own Funds 3, 3A-3J), deduct participations in financial and credit institutions (3K),
deduct restricted own funds above the RFF/MA notional SCR (3L), and

ELIGIBLE OWN FUNDS = own funds after applying the tier limits in Own Funds 4 / 4A,
   separately for covering the SCR and for covering the MCR.
```

Two separate coverage tests then run: **eligible own funds must cover the SCR**
(SCR-GP 2.1 *[SCR-GP, stream C]*) and **eligible own funds must cover the MCR** (MCR 2.1 [R78]).
The eligible-own-funds numerator is different in each because the tier limits differ.

### 2. Basic own funds and the reconciliation reserve [R77]

**Own Funds 2.1** — a firm's own funds comprise the sum of its basic own funds and, subject to
2.5, its ancillary own funds (Art. 87 SII).

**Own Funds 2.2** — basic own funds consist of (1) the excess of assets over liabilities, **less
the amount of own shares held by the firm**, and (2) subordinated liabilities (Art. 88 SII).

**Own Funds 3C.1 — the reconciliation reserve.** The reconciliation reserve is a *residual*: it
equals the total excess of assets over liabilities **reduced by all of** the following.

```
reconciliation reserve
  = total excess of assets over liabilities
  - own shares held by the firm                                            3C.1(1)
  - foreseeable dividends, distributions and charges                       3C.1(2)
  - the basic own funds items in 3A.1(1)(a)-(e), 3D.1(1) and 3F.1(1)       3C.1(3)
  - any basic own funds item not in those lists for which the firm holds
    a classification of own funds permission                               3C.1(4)
  - restricted own funds items that either
       (a) exceed the notional SCR of an MA portfolio or ring-fenced fund
           determined under 3L.1, or
       (b) are excluded under 3L.2                                         3C.1(5)
  - participations in financial and credit institutions per 3K.6 deducted
    under 3K, to the extent not already caught by (1)-(5)                  3C.1(6)
```

**3C.2** — the reconciliation reserve **may be positive or negative**. **3C.3** — a firm is *not*
required to determine whether, and to what extent, the reconciliation reserve displays the Tier 1
features in 3B by assessing the features of the underlying assets and liabilities or of the
underlying financial-statement items. That is the rule that lets the whole residual sit in Tier 1
unrestricted without a look-through: it is the item through which every actuarial modelling
decision (best estimate assumptions, contract boundaries, management actions, TMTP) reaches
regulatory capital.

Items 3A.1(1)(a)–(e) subtracted at 3C.1(3) are paid-in ordinary share capital and share premium;
paid-in initial funds / members' contributions / equivalent for mutuals; paid-in subordinated
mutual member accounts; **surplus funds not treated as insurance obligations under Surplus Funds
2.1 [R79]**; and paid-in preference shares and related share premium. So for a with-profits
insurer the estate leaves the reconciliation reserve through 3C.1(3) and re-enters own funds
directly as its own Tier 1 item under 3A.1(1)(d).

### 3. Ancillary own funds [R77][R83]

**2.3** — ancillary own funds are items *other than* basic own funds that can be called up to
absorb losses, including: unpaid share capital or initial fund not called up; letters of credit
and guarantees; any other legally binding commitments received by the firm; and, for a mutual,
future claims against members by way of a call for supplementary contribution **within the next
12 months** (Art. 89(1) SII).

**2.4** — once an AOF item is paid in or called up, the proceeds become an asset and the item
ceases to be ancillary own funds.

**2.5 / 2.6** — an AOF item may not be counted at all without an **ancillary own funds
permission** specifying either a monetary amount or a method (plus the amount determined by that
method for a specified period); the firm may only include the item up to that amount, and for
2.5(2) only for the specified period. **2.7** — the amount attributed must reflect the item's
loss-absorbency and be based on prudent and realistic assumptions.

**Tiering of AOF (3H–3J).** 3H.1 lists nine AOF items that are **Tier 2** if they display the 3I
features: unpaid and uncalled ordinary share capital / initial funds / preference shares, in each
case **callable on demand**; a legally binding commitment to subscribe and pay for subordinated
liabilities on demand; letters of credit and guarantees held in trust for insurance creditors by
an independent trustee and provided by credit institutions; other letters of credit and guarantees
callable on demand and clear of encumbrances; shipowners' mutual supplementary calls (RAO Sch.1
paras 6, 12, 17) within the following 12 months; other mutual supplementary calls callable on
demand and clear of encumbrances; and other legally binding commitments callable on demand and
clear of encumbrances. **3I.1** — an item on that list is **not** Tier 2 unless, once called up
and paid in, it would display the Tier 1 features under 3A and 3B. **3J.1** — an AOF item covered
by a permission but failing 3I is **Tier 3**.

SS2/15 para 2.1A [R83]: an AOF item **must be callable on demand** and the firm must show there
is no trigger event or restriction on when it can be called. SS2/15 para 2.1B: AOF is **not
emergency capital**; a firm in danger of breaching its SCR should raise basic own funds instead.
For a life insurer running a liability cash flow model, AOF is essentially out of scope: nothing
in a projection produces it.

### 4. The four tiers and the features each requires [R77]

**Gateway tests (3.1–3.6).** Tier 1 requires an item of *basic* own funds that substantially
possesses **both** characteristics in 3.5: (1) **permanent availability** — available, or callable
on demand, to fully absorb losses on a going-concern basis as well as in winding up; and (2)
**subordination** — in winding up the total amount is available to absorb losses and repayment is
refused until all other obligations, **including insurance and reinsurance obligations to
policyholders**, have been met (Art. 93(1) SII). Tier 2 requires characteristic (2) only for
basic own funds, or both for ancillary own funds. Tier 3 is the residual (3.3). In assessing
those characteristics, currently and in future, the firm must consider (3.6): **sufficient
duration** (dated or undated; if dated, duration relative to the duration of the firm's insurance
obligations), **absence of incentives to redeem**, **absence of mandatory servicing costs**, and
**absence of encumbrances**.

**Tier 1 unrestricted — the list (3A.1).** The part of the excess of assets over liabilities,
valued under Valuation 2 and the Technical Provisions Part, comprising:
(a) paid-in ordinary share capital and related share premium;
(b) paid-in initial funds, members' contributions or the mutual equivalent;
(c) paid-in subordinated mutual member accounts;
(d) **surplus funds not treated as insurance and reinsurance obligations under Surplus Funds
2.1**;
(e) paid-in preference shares and related share premium;
(f) **a reconciliation reserve calculated under 3C**;
plus (2) paid-in subordinated liabilities valued under Valuation 2.

**Tier 1 restricted (rT1).** Defined in 1.2 as the items in **3A.1(1)(c)**, **3A.1(1)(e)** and
**3A.1(2)** — i.e. paid-in subordinated mutual member accounts, paid-in preference shares, and
paid-in subordinated liabilities. Everything else in the 3A.1 list is Tier 1 *unrestricted*.

**Tier 1 features (3B.1), fifteen conditions.** The load-bearing ones: (1) ordinary shares /
initial funds rank after all other claims in winding up, while rT1 ranks **at the same level as or
ahead of** them but **after** Tier 2 (3D/3E) and Tier 3 (3F/3G) items and after all policyholders
and non-subordinated creditors; (3) the item is immediately available to absorb losses; (4) it
absorbs losses **at least once there is non-compliance with the SCR**; (5) rT1 must carry a
**principal loss absorbency mechanism** — write-down (3B.5, 3B.6), automatic conversion into
ordinary share capital or initial funds (3B.7, 3B.8), or an equivalent-outcome mechanism —
triggered at the 3B.10 trigger event; (6) ordinary shares / initial funds must be undated (or
match a fixed-maturity firm), and **rT1 must be undated or have its first contractual repayment
opportunity no earlier than five years from issuance**; (7) rT1 may only allow repayment between
**5 and 10 years** after issuance where the SCR is exceeded by an appropriate margin given the
firm's medium-term capital management plan; (8)–(10) repayment only at the firm's option, with
prior PRA permission, no incentives to redeem, and suspension of repayment on SCR non-compliance;
(11) the narrow carve-out permitting repayment during SCR non-compliance — prior PRA permission,
exchange or conversion into a Tier 1 item of at least the same quality, **and the MCR complied
with afterwards**; (12)–(13) equivalent rules for **cancellation of distributions** on SCR
non-compliance, with the same three-part carve-out; (14) **full flexibility over distributions**
per 3B.3 (ordinary shares: no preferential distribution treatment, distributions paid out of
distributable items, level not determined by issue price and no cap, no obligation to distribute,
non-payment is not an event of default, cancellation imposes no restrictions) or 3B.4 (rT1: full
discretion to cancel for an unlimited period on a non-cumulative basis, cancelled amounts usable
without restriction, no substitution, **no dividend pusher**, non-payment not an event of default,
cancellation imposes no restrictions); (15) free from encumbrances and not connected with any
transaction that would break 3.1.

**The rT1 trigger event (3B.9, 3B.10).** Loss absorbency from cancelling or reducing distributions
does **not** count as a principal loss absorbency mechanism (3B.9). The trigger event is
**significant non-compliance with the SCR**, which is met where **any** of:
```
(1) eligible own funds to cover the SCR  <=  75% of the SCR
(2) eligible own funds to cover the MCR  <=  the MCR
(3) compliance with the SCR is not re-established within three months
    of the date non-compliance was first observed
```
A firm may specify additional trigger events in the instrument's terms.

**Write-down mechanics (3B.6).** On a trigger under 3B.10(3) (the three-month condition): partial
write-down if that suffices to re-establish SCR compliance, otherwise write-down **at least on a
linear basis such that full write-down occurs when 75% coverage of the SCR is reached, or before**.
On a trigger under 3B.10(1) or (2) (the 75%-of-SCR or MCR-breach conditions): **full write-down**.
After an initial linear write-down, further write-downs follow **at the end of each subsequent
three-month period** for each further deterioration in the solvency ratio, where "solvency ratio"
is the ratio of eligible own funds covering the SCR to the SCR, until SCR compliance is restored.

**3B.11** — for the purposes of 3B.1(4), (10) and (12), references to the SCR are read as
references to the **MCR** where MCR non-compliance occurs before SCR non-compliance.

**3B.2** — exchange or conversion into another Tier 1 item, or repayment out of the proceeds of a
new item of at least the same quality, is **not** a repayment or redemption, provided prior PRA
permission is obtained.

**Tier 2 basic own funds (3D.1, 3E.1).** The list mirrors Tier 1 without the "paid-in"
requirement and without surplus funds or the reconciliation reserve: ordinary share capital and
premium; initial funds / members' contributions / mutual equivalent; subordinated mutual member
accounts; preference shares and premium; plus subordinated liabilities. Features (3E.1(1)–(10)):
ranks after all policyholders and non-subordinated creditors; **undated, or original maturity at
least 10 years, or first repayment opportunity not before 5 years** (3E.1(3)); repayment only at
the firm's option with prior PRA permission; **limited** incentives to redeem permitted provided
they do not occur before **10 years** from issuance (3E.1(5)); suspension of repayment on SCR
non-compliance with the same carve-out; **deferral** (not cancellation) of distributions on SCR
non-compliance with the same carve-out; free from encumbrances. **3E.1(11)** is an alternative
route: an item that has the rT1 features but **exceeds the 4A.3 limit** is Tier 2. **3E.4** — an
interest-rate step-up counts as a *limited* incentive to redeem only where it is a single
increase no greater than the higher of **100 basis points less the swap spread between the initial
and stepped-up index bases**, and **50% of the initial credit spread less that swap spread**.

**Tier 3 basic own funds (3F.1, 3G.1).** List: subordinated mutual member accounts; preference
shares and premium; **an amount equal to the value of net deferred tax assets**; plus subordinated
liabilities. Features: ranks after all policyholders and non-subordinated creditors; **undated or
original maturity at least five years**, the maturity date being the first contractual repayment
opportunity (3G.1(3)); repayment at the firm's option with prior PRA permission; limited
incentives to redeem permitted (3G.4 uses the same 100bp / 50%-of-credit-spread test as 3E.4);
suspension of repayment on SCR non-compliance with the usual carve-out; and **deferral of
distributions on MCR (not SCR) non-compliance** (3G.1(8)). **3G.3** — references to the SCR in
3G.1(6) are read as references to the MCR where MCR non-compliance comes first. **3G.5** permits
repayment before five years where the SCR will be exceeded by an appropriate margin afterwards
(remaining conditions of 3G.5 not transcribed).

For a UK life insurer the practical picture is: **Tier 1 unrestricted = share capital + share
premium + surplus funds + the reconciliation reserve; Tier 1 restricted = perpetual subordinated
debt and preference shares meeting 3B; Tier 2 = dated subordinated debt; Tier 3 = net deferred tax
assets** (3F.1(1)(c)) and any dated subordinated debt of 5–10 year original maturity that fails
the Tier 2 tests.

### 5. The participations deduction (3K) [R77]

**3K.1** — deduct from basic own funds the **full value** of a participation in a financial or
credit institution that **exceeds 10%** of the items in 3A.1(1)(a), (b), (d) and (f) — i.e. 10% of
(paid-in ordinary share capital + paid-in initial funds + **surplus funds** + reconciliation
reserve). **3K.2** — deduct the part of the aggregate value of all *other* such participations to
the extent it exceeds the same 10% base; **3K.4** applies that deduction pro rata. **3K.3** —
strategic participations within the meaning of SCR-SF 3D10 that are included in group solvency on
method 1 are **not** deducted. **3K.5** — the deduction is taken from the corresponding tier: CET1
holdings from 3A.1(1)(a), (b), (d), (f); AT1 holdings from the rT1 items; Tier 2 instrument
holdings from the 3D items. **3K.6** — "participations in financial and credit institutions"
covers participations in credit institutions, financial institutions and investment firms, plus
subordinated claims and CRR Articles 61–63 instruments held in those entities.

### 6. Eligibility and the tier limits — two chapters, two formulations [R77][R83]

**Chapter 4 (dated 01/01/2016, noting Art. 98 SII), expressed on eligible own funds:**
```
covering the SCR (4.1):  Tier 1 > one-third of total eligible own funds
                         Tier 3 < one-third of total eligible own funds
covering the MCR (4.2):  Tier 1 > 50% of eligible own funds
```

**Chapter 4A (dated 31/12/2024), expressed on the SCR and the MCR:**
```
covering the SCR (4A.1):  Tier 1          >= 50% of the SCR
                          Tier 3           < 15% of the SCR
                          Tier 2 + Tier 3 <= 50% of the SCR
covering the MCR (4A.2):  Tier 1          >= 80% of the MCR
                          Tier 2          <= 20% of the MCR
                          (Tier 3 is not eligible to cover the MCR at all)
```
**4A.3 — the restricted Tier 1 sub-limit.** For the purposes of 4A.1(1) and 4A.2(1), the sum of
(1) Tier 1 items displaying the **rT1** features in 3B and (2) items included in Tier 1 under the
transitional arrangement in **Transitional Measures 4.1** must make up **less than 20% of total
Tier 1 own funds items**. **4A.4** — the 4A limits also apply to items held under a classification
of own funds permission.

The two chapters are both live in the 05/08/2026 view and are **not arithmetically equivalent**
(one is a share of eligible own funds, the other a share of the requirement). SS2/15 para 1.3(d)
[R83] states that "**For the purposes of Own Funds 4, Own Funds 4A sets out the applicable
limits**", which is the PRA's own reconciliation. Recorded, not resolved — see Gaps.

**Transitional Measures 4.1** (grandfathering of pre-2016 instruments into Tier 1) is referenced
by 4A.3 but the **Transitional Measures Part was not retrieved for this stream**; its content is
`[unverified]` here. The Transitional Measures Part is partly covered by the discounting stream
as [R57] (Chapters 10 and 12 only).

### 7. Pre-issuance notification (Chapter 5) [R77][R83]

**5.1** — the chapter does not apply to items needing a classification of own funds permission or
an ancillary own funds permission. **5.2(1)** — notify the PRA in writing of an intention to issue
a basic own funds item **at least one month** before the intended issue date, unless exceptional
circumstances make that impracticable. **5.2(2)** — the notification must give the amount sought
and whether the issue is external or intra-group; the intended tier; **draft terms and
conditions**; a **draft reasoned independent legal opinion** that the item complies with the rules
for that tier; for any item within 4A.3, a **draft reasoned independent accounting opinion** on
its financial-statement treatment; **confirmation from the governing body**; and a statement of
encumbrances or connected transactions. **5.3** — notify changes to the submitted information
without delay. **5.4** — debt securities programmes must be notified at establishment and on
amendment, with the 5.2(2) information, at least one month before any drawdown. **5.5** — the
one-month notice does not apply to: further ordinary shares identical to shares previously issued
and meeting the Tier 1 ordinary share criteria; drawdowns under a programme notified within the
previous twelve months and unaffected by legal change; and items issued on terms identical to an
item issued and notified within the previous twelve months **other than as to issue date, maturity
date, amount, currency and interest rate**. **5.6** — those exempt issues still require written
notice **no later than the issue date**, with the 5.2 information minus the draft terms and the
two draft opinions. **5.7** — one month's notice is also required to amend or vary the terms of
an existing basic own funds item. **5.8** — after issuance the firm must provide the finalised
legal opinion, the finalised accounting opinion if applicable, the final terms and conditions, and
**a reasoned basis for the choice of coupon structure and any other provision that might suggest
an incentive to redeem**. SS2/15 para 5.1 [R83]: circumstances are unlikely to be "exceptional"
unless there is a risk of the firm failing to comply with its SCR or MCR if one month's notice is
observed.

### 8. Expected profits included in future premiums (EPIFP) — what is left of it

**There is no EPIFP rule in the Own Funds Part.** The retrieved Part (74,507 chars, full text) has
**zero occurrences of "expected profit"** [R77]. Nor is there one in the Technical Provisions
Part or the Technical Provisions – Further Requirements Part as retrieved by the sibling stream,
nor in the Reporting Part [R84].

The only live retrieved text of the concept is **assimilated EU law**: Commission Delegated
Regulation (EU) 2015/35 **Article 1(46)**, on legislation.gov.uk, shown as "up to date with all
changes known to be in force on or before 05 August 2026":

> "the expected profit included in future premiums" means the expected present value of future
> cash flows which result from the inclusion in technical provisions of premiums relating to
> existing insurance and reinsurance contracts that are expected to be received in the future, but
> that may not be received for any reason, other than because the insured event has occurred,
> regardless of the legal or contractual rights of the policyholder to discontinue the policy.

**PS3/24 [R86] is decisive on the UK reporting position.** Para 4.43 records that eight
respondents "welcomed the deletion of the expected profits included in future premiums (EPIFP)
requirement in S.23.01"; para 4.44 records that "The PRA has amended the template instructions to
clarify that the **EPIFP requirement is being removed from all reporting, including disclosure**."

Consequences for this library: **a UK own funds document must not describe an EPIFP disclosure**,
and a liability cash flow model built for Solvency UK does **not** need to produce the EPIFP
decomposition (recompute the BEL with future premiums set to nil and take the difference) that an
EU model still needs. The *economics* survive inside the reconciliation reserve — profit in future
premiums raises the excess of assets over liabilities and therefore the reconciliation reserve —
but no rule requires it to be isolated. See Gaps for what could not be settled: whether DR 2015/35
Article 1 remains operative UK law at all after PS15/24 [R6] revoked and restated the standard
formula articles.

### 9. The Minimum Capital Requirement [R78]

**Coverage duty.** MCR 2.1 — a firm must hold **eligible own funds covering the MCR** (Art. 128
SII). The eligible-own-funds numerator here is the one constrained by Own Funds 4.2 / 4A.2, i.e.
Tier 1 at least 80% of the MCR and Tier 2 at most 20%, **with Tier 3 not admissible at all**.

**The MCR itself (3.1A, 3.1B, 3.2, 3.3).**
```
MCR          = max( MCR_combined , AMCR )                               3.1A
MCR_combined = min( max( MCR_linear , 0.25 · SCR ) , 0.45 · SCR )       3.1B
MCR_linear   = MCR_linear_gi + MCR_linear_l                             3A.1
AMCR         = the absolute floor in 3.2
```
`SCR` in 3.1B is the SCR calculated under the SCR Rules. **3.3** adds, independently of the
absolute floor, that the MCR "must neither fall below 25% nor exceed 45% of the firm's SCR …
**including any capital add-on which has been imposed**" (Art. 129(3) SII). 3.3 is the only place
that says the corridor SCR is add-on-inclusive; 3.1B does not repeat it.

**The absolute floor (3.2), in sterling:**
```
general insurance                                          £2,400,000
  ... but if any of GI classes 10-15 are covered            £3,500,000
long-term insurance                                        £3,500,000
pure reinsurers                                            £3,500,000
  ... captive reinsurers that are pure reinsurers          £1,200,000
composites (not pure reinsurers)      sum of the GI and long-term amounts
  ... except: a composite whose GI permission is limited to class 1
      (accident) or class 2 (sickness) and where GI or long-term gross
      written premiums do not exceed 10% of the firm's total gross
      written premiums                                     £3,500,000
```
For every product in this library the relevant floor is **£3,500,000** (long-term insurance).

**The long-term linear formula (3C.1) — this is the life MCR.**
```
MCR_linear_l = 0.037 · TP_l1
             - 0.052 · TP_l2
             + 0.007 · TP_l3
             + 0.021 · TP_l4
             + 0.0007 · CAR
```
where each `TP` is **technical provisions without a risk margin**, net of reinsurance and SPV
recoverables, **floored at zero** for each term separately:

- **TP_l1** — in relation to **guaranteed benefits** for long-term obligations **with profit
  participation**, plus TPs for reinsurance obligations where the underlying long-term obligations
  include profit participation.
- **TP_l2** — in relation to **future discretionary benefits** for long-term obligations with
  profit participation. **This is the only negative coefficient in the formula**: a larger FDB
  reserve *reduces* MCR_linear.
- **TP_l3** — for **linked long-term liabilities**.
- **TP_l4** — for all other long-term insurance and reinsurance obligations.
- **CAR** — total **capital at risk**: the sum, over each contract giving rise to long-term
  obligations, of `max(0, A - B)` where
  ```
  A = (i)  the amount the firm would currently pay on death or disability of the
           persons insured, net of reinsurance and SPV recoverables
    + (ii) the expected present value of amounts not covered in (i) that the firm
           would pay in future on the immediate death or disability of the persons
           insured, net of reinsurance and SPV recoverables
  B = the best estimate of the corresponding obligations, net of reinsurance and
      SPV recoverables
  ```
  i.e. **net amount at risk**, floored at zero **per contract** (the floor is on the contract, not
  on the portfolio sum).

**3C.2** — the reinsurance/SPV recoverables netted off in 3C.1(1)–(4) must exclude amounts that
cannot be taken into account under Technical Provisions – Further Requirements 23.3 and 23.5, and
amounts from arrangements that do not comply with SCR-SF 3G2, 3G3, 3G5, 3G6, 3G7, 3G8 and 3G9
[R112] or with SCR – Internal Models 11.10 to 11.12 [R81].

**The general insurance linear component (3B.1)** is `sum over segments s of (alpha_s · TP_gi_s +
beta_s · P_s)`, with the sixteen-segment factor table in MCR 6.1 (factors range from 4.7% for
medical expense to 18.6% for several segments; **income protection insurance as a general
insurance segment carries alpha = 13.1% and beta = 8.5%** — relevant only if IP is written as
general insurance class 2/14 rather than as long-term business). Not otherwise transcribed: this
library's products are long-term business.

**Frequency and reporting (4.1, 4.2).** The MCR must be calculated **at least quarterly** and
reported to the PRA under Reporting 2.1–2.5 [R84]. Where **either corridor limit determines the
MCR** (i.e. the 25% floor or the 45% cap binds), the firm must provide the PRA with information
allowing a proper understanding of the reasons (4.2).

**Practical shape for a life insurer.** MCR_linear for a UK life book is almost always far below
25% of the SCR, so **the MCR is normally the 25% collar: MCR = 0.25 · SCR**. The linear formula
matters mainly (a) as the "pre-corridor MCR" the PRA uses as a model-drift metric (SS15/16 para
2.3 [R81c]), and (b) for very small or run-off entities where the £3.5m floor or the 45% cap can
bind.

### 11. Ring-fenced funds: definition, the own-funds deduction, and the SCR adjustment

**What makes a fund ring-fenced.** Glossary [R80]: a **ring-fenced fund** is "an identifiable unit
of assets and liabilities where the existence of a restriction on those assets in relation to
those liabilities on a going concern basis gives rise to **restricted own funds**, **other than a
matching adjustment portfolio**". **Restricted own funds** are own funds items with reduced
capacity to absorb losses on a going-concern basis because of lack of transferability within the
firm, where (1) they can only cover losses on a defined portion of the firm's contracts of
insurance, (2) they can only cover losses in respect of certain policyholders, or (3) they can
only cover losses arising from particular risks or liabilities — **"but does not include the value
of future transfers attributable to shareholders"**. That last clause is the reason a with-profits
fund's expected future shareholder transfer is *not* trapped in the ring fence.

**The UK application is categorical for with-profits.** SS14/15 para 2.2 [R80b]: "the PRA expects
that **such restrictions will generally mean that each with-profits fund displays the
characteristics of a RFF**", and the firm must therefore "reflect the lack of availability of
assets and own funds within the with-profits fund to cover the risks of the rest of the firm".
Para 2.3: a **sub-fund** that must be treated as a separate with-profits fund under COBS 20 [R9]
is expected to be treated as **a separate RFF**. So a multi-fund with-profits insurer runs the
whole apparatus below once per fund.

**The own-funds deduction (Own Funds 3L) [R77].**
```
3L.1  For the purposes of calculating the reconciliation reserve, reduce the excess
      of assets over liabilities by
          max( 0 , restricted own funds items within the RFF or MA portfolio
                   - notional SCR of that RFF or MA portfolio )
      Standard formula firms: notional SCR computed under SCR-SF 9.1 [R112].
      Internal model firms:   notional SCR computed using the internal model, as if
                              the firm pursued only the business in that RFF or MA
                              portfolio.
3L.2  Where the assets, liabilities and risk within a ring-fenced fund are NOT
      material, the firm may instead reduce the reconciliation reserve by the TOTAL
      amount of restricted own funds items.
```
Economically: **restricted own funds count towards group-level own funds only up to the amount of
capital the ring-fenced fund itself needs.** Any excess — the classic "surplus estate above the
fund's own SCR" — is deducted from the reconciliation reserve and does not support the rest of the
firm. Own Funds 3C.1(5) is the corresponding subtraction line.

**The SCR adjustment (SCR-SF 2.2 and Chapter 9) [R112].** SCR-SF 2.2: a firm with an RFF (**other
than one where the reconciliation reserve has been reduced by the total amount of restricted own
funds under Own Funds 3L.2**) or an MA portfolio must adjust its SCR by the SCR-SF 9 method. That
carve-out is the pay-off for electing the 3L.2 simplification: full own-funds deduction, no
notional SCR.

SCR-SF **9.1**, verified in full:
1. calculate a **notional SCR for each RFF, each MA portfolio and the remaining part of the firm**,
   as if each were a separate firm;
2. **the firm's SCR is the sum of those notional SCRs**;
3. where a module or sub-module capital requirement is scenario-based, calculate the scenario
   impact on basic own funds **at the level of** each RFF, each MA portfolio and the remaining
   part;
4. at RFF / MA-portfolio level, basic own funds for that purpose **include only restricted own
   funds**;
5. where profit participation arrangements exist in the RFF: if the scenario *increases* RFF basic
   own funds, adjust the increase down by the increase in technical provisions from the increase in
   future discretionary benefits the firm would expect to pay in that scenario; if the scenario
   *decreases* them, adjust the decrease (for the net basic SCR under SCR-SF 6.3(2)) by the
   reduction in future discretionary benefits, **capped at the FDB included in the technical
   provisions of that RFF**;
6. notwithstanding (1), compute each notional SCR using **the scenario under which the basic own
   funds of the firm as a whole are most negatively affected**;
7. to find that scenario, sum the scenario impacts at each RFF and MA-portfolio level per (3) and
   (5) and add them to one another and to the impact on the remaining part;
8. determine each notional SCR by aggregating the sub-module and module requirements of the basic
   SCR;
9. **no diversification effects are allowed between an RFF, an MA portfolio, and the remaining
   part of the firm.**

Rule 9.1 is date-stamped **24/07/2025** (there is a "past version before 24/07/2025"; the earlier
text was not retrieved).

**The two effects, stated separately, because they are often conflated.**
- *Numerator effect* (Own Funds 3L): restricted own funds above the RFF's notional SCR are struck
  out of the reconciliation reserve.
- *Denominator effect* (SCR-SF 9.1(2), 9.1(9)): the entity SCR is the **sum** of notional SCRs,
  so the diversification credit that a single-entity calculation would give between the
  with-profits fund and the shareholder fund is **lost**.
A UK with-profits insurer therefore takes the ring-fencing hit twice, and a model must be able to
run the whole standard formula (or internal model) **once per ring-fenced fund plus once for the
remaining part**, with a consistent scenario selected at entity level under 9.1(6)–(7).

### 11a. The EIOPA ring-fenced funds guidelines, as applied in the UK [R80c]

**Read every reference below with the status caveat in the R80c entry: the guidelines cite the
Solvency II Directive and Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/35 articles, not PRA rules, and their
continued application in the UK rests on SoP1/19 (not retrieved).** They are the only retrieved
document that says *how* to identify a ring-fenced fund and *how* to run the notional SCR, so
they are transcribed with that health warning.

**Guideline 1 — the defining characteristic.** An RFF is identified by: (a) a restriction on
assets in relation to certain liabilities on a going-concern basis that leads to restricted own
funds — **this is the defining characteristic**; (b) RFFs may arise **with or without** profit
participation; (c) the assets and liabilities must form an identifiable unit, **but need not be
managed together as a separate unit or form a separate sub-fund**; (d) where the proceeds of, or
returns on, the assets are also subject to the arrangement, the firm must be able to **trace them
at any given time**.

**Guideline 2 — generally outside the scope of RFF treatment:** (a) **conventional unit-linked
products**; (b) **conventional index-linked products**; (c) provisions set up in accounts or
financial statements (including equalisation provisions) are not RFFs solely by being so set up;
(d) conventional reinsurance business, provided individual contracts do not restrict the firm's
assets; (e) coverage assets and similar winding-up protections including the special register;
(f) the separation of life and non-life business in composites (though an RFF may still arise
within either part); (g) **"surplus funds are not ring-fenced solely by virtue of being surplus
funds, but could be if they are generated within a ring-fenced fund"**; (h) temporary
national-law separation of a transferred-in portfolio during a business reorganisation; and (i)
**experience funds**, where policyholders are entitled to a share of fund experience by a
predefined mechanism set out in the policy documentation with no rights to unallocated amounts —
allocated amounts go into technical provisions and unallocated amounts are fully transferable and
therefore unrestricted own funds.

Guideline 2(a) and 2(g) together are the pair that decide the UK product picture: **a
unit-linked bond is not ring-fenced**, and **surplus funds are ring-fenced only because they arise
inside a with-profits fund**, not because they are surplus funds.

**Guideline 4 — the archetypal with-profits RFF.** A fund of assets and liabilities in respect of
profit-participation business available only to cover losses of particular policyholders or risks,
where: (i) policyholders in the fund have **distinct rights** relative to other business; (ii)
there are restrictions on using the assets, and returns on them, to meet liabilities or losses
arising **outside** the fund; (iii) **an excess of assets over liabilities is generally maintained
within the fund and that excess is restricted own funds**; and (iv) there is generally profit
participation whereby policyholders receive a minimum proportion of the profits generated in the
fund, with shareholders receiving the balance. Other listed types: trust or legally binding
arrangements for the benefit of specified policyholders; restrictions in the firm's constitution;
restrictions in national law; and occupational-retirement-provision ring-fencing. ¶1.15 —
**Guidelines 6 to 17 apply to matching adjustment portfolios** because of their reduced
transferability and reduced scope for diversification.

**Guideline 5 — materiality.** Where an RFF is not material the firm may exclude the **total**
amount of restricted own funds items from own funds eligible to cover **the SCR and the MCR**, and
is then **not required to calculate a notional SCR**; but it must include the fund's assets and
liabilities in the remaining part for the overall SCR calculation. (This is the guideline behind
Own Funds 3L.2 and the SCR-SF 2.2 carve-out.) Materiality is assessed on: the risks arising from
or covered by the fund; its assets and liabilities; **the amount of restricted own funds, its
volatility over time, and its proportion of total own funds**; the proportion of total assets and
capital requirements the fund represents individually or with other RFFs; and the likely impact on
the SCR from reduced diversification.

**Guidelines 6 and 7 — what is inside.** Assets = any specified assets or pools of assets **and
any related cash flows** restricted by the arrangement. Liabilities = only those properly
attributable to the policies or risks covered, or those the restricted assets can be used for;
and for profit-participation business the best estimate liabilities **must include any future
discretionary benefits the firm expects to pay**. ¶1.20 — **the valuation of the liabilities used
for RFF calculations must be the same as would have been derived if they were not in an RFF**
(no separate RFF valuation basis).

**Guideline 8 — future shareholder transfers.** They are (a) relevant **only** for profit
participation business; (b) they **come into existence when the corresponding future discretionary
benefits are recognised in the best estimate liability**; (c) they are **part of the RFF's excess
of assets over liabilities, not a liability of the RFF**; and (d) they include transfers relating
to **declared bonuses already inside guaranteed benefits** where the corresponding shareholder
distribution has not yet been transferred out. This is the guideline behind the closing words of
the *restricted own funds* glossary definition [R80].

**Guideline 9 — the notional SCR under the standard formula.** (a) apply the SCR methodology to
the fund's assets and liabilities as if it were a separate undertaking, **including an operational
risk capital requirement and any relevant loss-absorbing-capacity adjustments for technical
provisions and deferred taxes**; (b) aggregate the sub-module and module requirements under the
**worst case scenario for the undertaking as a whole**, using the standard formula aggregation —
**diversification within the fund may be recognised**; (c) the fund-level requirement is
calculated **net of the mitigating effect of future discretionary benefits**, with realistic
assumptions on the variation of future bonus rates having due regard to the impact of the shock at
fund level **including the impact on the value of future shareholder transfers** and to any
contractual, legal or statutory constraints on the profit participation mechanism; (d) where a
bidirectional scenario produces a **negative** worst-case charge even after allowing for profit
participation, **set the charge to zero**.

**Guideline 10 — the notional SCR under an internal model.** The model must be **capable of
calculating the notional SCR for each RFF as if it were a separate undertaking pursuing only the
business in that fund**; each notional SCR must be **consistent with** the whole-firm SCR
calculation; the **risk-mitigation techniques and future management actions** used at fund level
must be consistent with those used for that business in the whole-firm calculation and with
Guideline 9; methodology and assumptions must be consistent with those used for the same types of
assets, liabilities and risks firm-wide; and differences may be used **only where necessary to
produce a compliant notional SCR, with the justification documented**.

**Guideline 11 — the comparison.** ¶1.25 — the effect of the adjustment is "to allow only an
amount of own funds equal to the notional SCR to contribute to the coverage of the SCR of the
undertaking as a whole **and to the coverage of the MCR**". ¶1.26 — **where own funds inside the
fund are equal to or less than its notional SCR, no adjustment is made** and all of them are
available to meet the SCR and MCR. Note that ¶1.25 answers a question the PRA rules do not: the
deduction bites on **MCR** coverage as well as SCR coverage.

**Guideline 12 — the whole-firm SCR.** ¶1.27 the remaining part is treated as a separate
undertaking and Guideline 9 applied to it; ¶1.28 **no diversification benefit among RFFs or
between RFFs and the remaining part**; ¶1.29 — **set any negative notional SCR to zero before
aggregating**.

**Guideline 13 — internal model evidence.** The firm must consider how each notional SCR is
calculated and how its diversification-measurement system reflects RFF restrictions, and must
give the supervisor: the nature of the business in each RFF and how it compares with other RFFs
and the remaining part; the degree of correlation of the risks attaching to those lines of
business; **historical data demonstrating the incidence of losses affecting different parts of the
business**; the rationale for and nature of the restrictions; an explanation of the source of
diversification and the **key variables driving dependencies**; an analysis of **non-linear
dependence and material lack of diversification under extreme scenarios**; and the extent to
which that data supports the observed diversification effects. (SCR-IM 11.9 [R81] is the PRA rule
that carries this.)

**Guideline 14** — the same calculation methodology may be applied to multiple similar RFFs only
where the firm can demonstrate to the supervisor that it produces **sufficiently accurate results
for each** of them. **Guidelines 15–16** — ongoing assessment obligations on the firm and the
supervisor where circumstances change so as to affect whether the reduction of diversification is
appropriately reflected in the model's outputs; the firm must determine whether an internal model
change is needed.

**Guideline 17 — reporting the SCR split by risk module.** A standard formula firm with RFFs or MA
portfolios should, when reporting and disclosing the SCR split by risk module, **identify the
effects of non-diversification** by allocating across risk modules the difference between (i) the
sum of notional SCRs and (ii) the SCR of the undertaking **as if there were no loss of
diversification**. The Technical Annex gives simplifications; the approach must be applied
consistently over time. **Simplification 1 (direct summation at sub-module level)**: for each
sub-module of the life, non-life, health, market and counterparty default modules, sum the gross
capital charges across all RFFs and the remaining part; then aggregate the sub-module results into
module results using the relevant correlation matrices. (The remaining simplifications in the
Technical Annex were **not transcribed**.) The article references are to DR 2015/35 Articles
311(2)(a) and 297(2)(b), i.e. EU reporting and SFCR articles, so the UK counterpart is a question
for the reporting stream [R84][R85].

### 12. Surplus funds: how a with-profits estate becomes Tier 1 capital

This is the single most load-bearing capital fact for the with-profits product. The chain, rule by
rule:

1. **Technical Provisions 9.1(3) [R1]** — in calculating technical provisions a firm must take into
   account "all payments to policyholders, including future discretionary bonuses, which firms
   expect to make, whether or not those payments are contractually guaranteed, **unless those
   payments fall within Surplus Funds 2.1**" (Art. 78 SII). Verified from the retrieved TP Part
   text.
2. **Surplus Funds 2.1 [R79]** — a firm "shall not treat surplus funds as insurance and
   reinsurance obligations when valuing payments to policyholders and beneficiaries in the
   calculation of technical provisions in accordance with Technical Provisions 2" (Art. 78(3) and
   Art. 91(2) SII).
3. **SS13/15 para 2.1 [R79b]** — that carve-out applies "where those surplus funds meet the
   requirements for Tier 1 own funds in Own Funds 3.1".
4. **Own Funds 3A.1(1)(d) [R77]** — "surplus funds that are not considered as insurance and
   reinsurance obligations in accordance with 2.1 of the Surplus Funds Part" are a **Tier 1
   (unrestricted) own funds item in their own right**, provided they display the 3B features.
5. **SS13/15 para 2.3 [R79b]** — "The PRA expects that surplus funds will normally meet the
   criteria for classification as Tier 1 own funds", **but** because of FCA policyholder-fairness
   rules "such surplus funds are likely to be treated as part of a ring-fenced [fund]". So they
   are Tier 1 **and** restricted own funds, and §11 applies to them.

**The calculation (Surplus Funds 3.1).** Per with-profits fund:
```
surplus funds =   value of with-profits assets                              3.1(1)
                - value of with-profits policy liabilities                  3.1(2)
                - tax and other costs arising on recognition of future
                  shareholder transfers properly attributable to the fund
                  under the FCA Handbook, to the extent not in 3.1(2)       3.1(3)
                - other liabilities properly attributable to the fund
                  (not attributable to policies in the fund)                3.1(4)
                - the value of future shareholder transfers relating to
                  with-profits policies in the fund which may properly be
                  made out of it under the FCA Handbook, valued by
                  reference to the 3.5(3) benefits and consistently with
                  the 3.4 method                                            3.1(5)
```
*with-profits assets* (1.2) = the assets in a with-profits fund **except those meeting liabilities
in respect of non-profit insurance**.

**Valuing with-profits policy liabilities — retrospective by default (3.2, 3.3).** Value them as
the aggregate **retrospective** value per policy (an asset share), treating items that increase the
future liability as positive and items that reduce it as negative:
```
(1)  premiums received
(2)  investment income and increases (decreases) in asset values
(3)  amounts representing permanent enhancements the firm will take into account
     when determining benefits ultimately payable
(4)  past miscellaneous surplus (or deficit) allocated
(5)  expenses incurred (or deductions in respect of expenses)
(6)  past deductions for the cost of guarantees and smoothing, options, life cover
     and any other benefits provided
(7)  partial benefits paid or due (other than benefits deducted under (6))
(8)  tax paid or payable properly attributable to that policy under the FCA Handbook
(9)  amounts received (paid) under reinsurance or economically similar arrangements
     relating to that policy (other than benefits deducted under (6))
(10) past shareholder transfers properly attributable to that policy under the FCA
     Handbook, LESS any implicit allowance for the value of future shareholder
     transfers
```

**Prospective only where retrospective fails (3.2, 3.4).** The prospective basis is required where
the retrospective value "does not adequately reflect the value of some or all of such liabilities"
or the firm can demonstrate the retrospective basis is impracticable. Prospective = aggregate net
present value of: future premiums; expenses expected to be incurred or deducted; planned
deductions for the cost of guarantees, smoothing, options, life cover and other benefits; benefits
payable of the 3.5 type; reinsurance amounts receivable/payable excluding benefits already allowed
for under (3) or previously deducted; and tax payable attributable to the policy under the FCA
Handbook.

**3.5 — which benefits go into the prospective calculation:** (1) all guaranteed benefits including
death and maturity guarantees, guaranteed surrender values and paid-up values; (2) declared
bonuses to which the policyholder is contractually entitled; and (3) **future discretionary
additions and discretionary payments expected to be made — but only to the extent they are
consistent with what the retrospective calculation under 3.3 would have allowed for.** That
consistency clamp is what stops the prospective basis from being used to inflate policyholder
liabilities and shrink the estate.

**3.6** — no charge may be attributed to a with-profits policy for these purposes unless the FCA
Handbook permits it. **4.1** — the valuations feeding 3.1 must be **consistent with the
methodology used for technical provisions under Technical Provisions 2** [R1].

**Four PRA expectations that change the numbers [R79b]:**
- **No risk margin in the surplus funds calculation** (para 2.4). The Surplus Funds Part "does not
  refer to or include a risk margin"; the risk margin remains a technical-provisions item for the
  business as a whole including with-profits. So **surplus funds and technical provisions are not
  a clean partition of the with-profits fund** — the risk margin sits on the TP side and is not
  netted off surplus funds.
- **Whole-of-life is the named example for the prospective basis** (para 3.1): whole-of-life
  policies, or policies where the retrospective calculation might be negative or significantly
  lower than the prospective value, are where the prospective approach may be necessary.
- **Grouping is allowed with three conditions** (para 3.2): approximations must be likely to give
  the same or a **higher** result than a policy-by-policy calculation; grouping must not materially
  misrepresent exposure or misstate the cost of guarantees, options or smoothing; and groupings
  must be of policies with similar attributes **including the status of guarantees**. Para 3.3:
  reassess groupings each time surplus funds are calculated.
- **Estate distributions are excluded from projected benefits** (para 3.6): the PRA "would not
  expect a firm to include within benefits payable distributions from the estate that it might
  make over the life of the with-profits policies were the with-profits fund in run-off, or that
  it expects to make if it is currently in run-off". This is what keeps the estate in own funds
  rather than in technical provisions. Para 3.7: firms' current expectations as to an affordable,
  prudent and realistic level of distributions should nonetheless inform projected FDB.

**Definitions of terms used above:** "permanent enhancements" (3.3(3)) means amounts previously
added to with-profits policy liabilities that the firm expects to be permanent — "to apply in all
but the most extreme adverse circumstances" (para 3.4); "miscellaneous surplus" (3.3(4)) captures
surplus or deficit from the experience of the fund, e.g. mortality or expense experience relative
to expectations, or **profits or losses on non-profit business written inside the with-profits
fund** (para 3.5).

**With-Profits Part obligations that constrain the estate [R80]:** 2.1 — hold assets in each
with-profits fund sufficient to cover the with-profits policy liabilities of all business written
in or transferred into it; 3.1 — the distribution strategy for discretionary benefits must be
**affordable and sustainable** and must not reasonably be expected to have an adverse effect on
the safety and soundness of the firm as a whole or on the benefit security of all policyholders;
4.1 — support arrangements (capital support from outside the fund) must be documented, including
when they take effect, repayment terms, and any restriction on the firm's use of them.

### 13. Internal models: permission and lifecycle [R81]

**Permission (2.1, 2.2).** A firm may calculate its SCR using a full or partial internal model
**only if** it has an internal model permission and **only to the extent of** that permission; a
firm that has one **must** use it (Art. 112(1)–(2) SII). Permissions are granted under s.138BA
FSMA (SS15/16 para 1.1 [R81c]).

**Application (3.1–3.4).** The applicant must either (a) confirm in writing and submit at minimum
documentary evidence demonstrating that the model and, where relevant, the firm satisfies
**SCR-GP 3.3 to 3.4** *[SCR-GP, stream C]* and the internal model requirements, or (b) identify
which of those requirements are not satisfied, explain why and in what way, and evidence
compliance with all the others (3.1). The firm must also demonstrate that its systems for
identifying, measuring, monitoring, managing and reporting risk are adequate (3.2), and submit its
**internal model change policy** with the application (3.3). **3.4 — a firm with an internal model
permission must be able, on PRA request, to provide an estimate of the SCR determined under the
standard formula** (Art. 112(7) SII). SS15/16 para 3.5 [R81c] turns that into a standing
capability expectation.

**Partial models (4.1, 4.2).** The internal model requirements apply with any changes necessary
for the limited scope (4.1). The applicant must (1) explain and properly justify the reason for
the limited scope; (2) explain how the resulting SCR **reflects more appropriately the risk
profile of the firm** and complies with SCR-GP 2 to 4; and (3) demonstrate that the design is
consistent with the SCR-GP 2–4 principles **so as to allow the capital requirement generated by
the partial model to be fully integrated into the standard formula SCR**.

**Scope transitional plans (5, 5A).** 5.1/5.2 — a partial-model applicant covering only certain
sub-modules of a risk module, or only some business units within a risk module, must be able on
request to submit a realistic plan to **extend** scope so that the model covers "a predominant
part of the firm's insurance business with respect to that specific risk module". 5A.1 (new,
31/12/2024) — where a firm is failing or likely to fail 5B.1, 10.3 or SCR-GP 5.1, it must be able
on request to submit a realistic plan to **reduce** the scope of its model so it no longer covers
the major business units or sub-modules where deficiencies arise.

**Internal model safeguards (1.2, 5B).** A *safeguard* is a limitation or requirement imposed by
the PRA — in the permission, under s.55M FSMA or otherwise — that either ensures compliance with
SCR-GP 3.3–3.4 where a **residual model limitation** relates to those provisions, or mitigates the
effect of a residual model limitation relating to the internal model requirements. 5B.1 — the
firm must make **all reasonable efforts** to remedy the residual limitation; 5B.2 — progress
reports on request.

**Model change (6).** 6.1 — a firm must not change its model otherwise than in accordance with the
model change policy covered by its permission. 6.2 — the policy must specify **minor and major**
changes and, where the firm applies **model limitation adjustments**, explain the governance for
their application including whether they are minor or major and why. 6.3 — no **major** change,
and no change to the change policy (other than the administrative changes allowed by 6.4), without
prior PRA approval to vary the permission. 6.5 — such applications follow the Chapters 3–5
procedure. 6.6 — where the existing permission modifies any internal model requirement, the firm
must also evidence and confirm/explain by reference to the **unmodified** requirements. **6.7 — a
transitional to 31 December 2026**: a firm applying model limitation adjustments may until then
change its change policy solely to document procedures for applying, reviewing and removing those
adjustments without prior approval, but must apply to vary its permission **before 31 December
2026**.

**Governing body (7).** The permission application, and any application to vary it for a major
model change, must be **approved by the firm's governing body** (7.1). The firm must have systems
ensuring the model **operates properly on a continuous basis** (7.2).

**No reversion (8.1).** A firm with an internal model permission **must not revert to calculating
the whole or any part of the SCR under the standard formula** in respect of that model (Art. 117
SII). Combined with 3.4 and SS15/16 para 3.5, the firm must be able to compute the standard
formula but must not use it.

**Non-compliance (9.1).** If a firm ceases to comply with the internal model requirements it must
**without delay** either present a plan to restore compliance within a reasonable period, or
demonstrate that the effect of non-compliance is **immaterial**.

### 14. The use test [R81]

**10.1** — the firm must demonstrate that the model is **widely used and plays an important role**
in its system of governance (Conditions Governing Business 2–7 [R92], Insurance – Fitness and
Propriety 2.1–2.3, 4.1, 4.3, 4.4, Insurance – Allocation of Responsibilities 4 [R94]) and
particularly in (1) its risk-management system (CGB 3.1–3.7) and decision-making processes, and
(2) its economic and solvency capital assessment and allocation processes **including its ORSA**
(CGB 3.8–3.11) [R92][R95].

**10.1A (new)** — a model is **not** widely used or important "where the quantifications of risks
and the risk ranking produced by the internal model **do not trigger timely and appropriate risk
management actions**, where relevant".

**10.2** — the frequency of SCR calculation using the model must be consistent with the frequency
with which the model is used for the 10.1 purposes. **10.10** allows a **simplified SCR
calculation** for meeting 10.2 — carrying out only part of the usual calculation — where the firm
uses results from the previous SCR calculation for the remainder, can demonstrate on request that
those carried-forward results would not be materially different from a fresh calculation, and does
**not** use the simplification for SCR-GP 4 (the annual/ad-hoc recalculation duties). *This is the
rule that authorises proxy or roll-forward models for interim capital reporting, and it is the
single most important internal-model rule for a liability cash flow modelling library.*

**10.3** — ongoing appropriateness of design and operation, and continued reflection of the firm's
risk profile. **10.4** — the design must be aligned with the firm's activities: (1) outputs
sufficiently granular to play an important role in relevant management decisions, and (2) **as a
minimum, outputs must differentiate between lines of business, between risk categories and between
major business units**. **10.5** — the change policy must provide for the model to be adjusted for
changes in the scope or nature of the firm's activities. **10.6** — where the firm decides **not**
to use the model for a part of the system of governance it must notify the PRA and justify that
decision. **10.7** — consistency between different outputs where the model is used for different
purposes.

**10.8 / 10.9 — board understanding.** The governing body and any other persons who effectively
run the firm must have sufficient understanding comprising: the structure of the model and how it
fits the business and is integrated in risk management; the scope and purposes and **which risks
are and are not covered**; the general methodology; **the limitations of the model**; the
**diversification effects** taken into account; and the **material expert judgements** used to set
assumptions. Persons who effectively run the firm need a sufficiently detailed understanding of
the parts used in their own area.

### 15. Statistical quality standards [R81]

**11.2** — methods used to calculate the **probability distribution forecast** must be based on
adequate, applicable and relevant actuarial and statistical techniques; based on current and
credible information and realistic assumptions making adequate allowance for uncertainty; and
**consistent with the methods used to calculate technical provisions**, except where that would
breach 11.6 (full coverage of material risks). **11.3** — assumptions must be justifiable to the
PRA.

**11.4 — data.** Data must be **accurate, complete and appropriate**. 11.4(1A): data are complete
only where data are available for **all relevant model parameters** and no relevant data are
excluded without justification. 11.4(2): data sets used in calculating the probability
distribution forecast must be updated **at least annually**, and data collected, processed and
applied in a transparent and structured manner.

**11.5** — irrespective of method, the model's **ability to rank risk** must be sufficient for
wide use and an important role in governance and capital allocation.

**11.6 / 11.6A / 11.6B — coverage.** The model must cover **all material risks**, including at
least the risks in SCR-GP 3.3(1) (non-life underwriting, life underwriting, health underwriting,
market, credit and operational risk). 11.6A: the firm must assess **at least quarterly** whether
the model covers all material quantifiable risks within its scope, using an appropriate set of
qualitative and quantitative indicators. 11.6B: the qualitative indicators must include **any
risks identified in the ORSA that are not included in the coverage of the model** [R95].

**11.7 — options and guarantees.** The model must accurately assess the particular risks
associated with **financial guarantees and any contractual options** where material, and the risks
associated with **both policyholder options and the firm's contractual options**, taking into
account the impact that future changes in financial and non-financial conditions may have on their
exercise; and must take account of **all payments to policyholders which the firm expects to make,
whether or not contractually guaranteed** (Art. 121(7), (9) SII). For this library this is the rule
that makes with-profits guarantees/smoothing, unit-linked guarantees, IP claim-inception options
and GAOs internal-model content rather than an add-on.

**11.8 — what may be recognised.** Only: (1) diversification effects, dependencies within and
across risk categories, **if the firm's measurement system is adequate**; (2) the effect of
risk-mitigation techniques, **if** credit risk and other risks arising from their use are properly
reflected per 11.10; and (3) **future management actions**, if and to the extent that they are
actions the firm would reasonably expect to carry out in specific circumstances **consistently
with Technical Provisions – Further Requirements 8.1–8.5 applied in the internal model context**,
and the model allows for the **time and expense** necessary to implement them.

**11.9 — adequacy of the diversification system.** Adequate only where it identifies the key
variables driving dependencies and takes into account (a) **non-linear dependence and lack of
diversification under extreme scenarios**, (b) **restrictions on diversification arising from the
existence of a ring-fenced fund or matching adjustment portfolio**, and (c) the characteristics of
the risk measure used. (b) is the internal-model counterpart of SCR-SF 9.1(9); note that Own Funds
3L.1 separately requires the **notional SCR of an RFF or MA portfolio to be computed using the
internal model, as if the firm pursued only the business in that fund or portfolio**.

**11.10–11.12 — risk mitigation.** Risks must **not** be recognised where (1) the contractual
arrangements are not legally effective and enforceable in any relevant jurisdiction, or do not
ensure the risk transfer is clearly defined and incontrovertible; (2) the firm has no direct claim
on the counterparty on default, insolvency, bankruptcy or another credit event set out in the
documentation; or (3) the legal arrangements contain no explicit reference to a specific risk
exposure clearly defining the extent of cover. Where cover under (3) does not extend to all cases,
the model must take account of the **reduced effectiveness** from that deviation (11.11); where a
technique is subject to a condition outside the firm's direct control that could undermine
effective risk transfer, the model must take account of the condition and any reduced
effectiveness (11.12).

**11.13 — third-party model parts and data.** Where the model uses parts obtained from a third
party, the model is adequate only if the firm can demonstrate **sufficient understanding of those
parts including their limitations**, such that it can provide meaningful challenge and explain how
they enable compliance with the internal model requirements and SCR-GP 3.3 and 3.4. Where it uses
third-party **data**, those data are appropriate only if the firm can demonstrate sufficient
understanding of them including their limitations. (E.g. a bought-in economic scenario generator,
or CMI tables and projections [R22]–[R31].)

### 16. Calibration standards [R81]

**12.2** — the firm must derive the SCR **directly from the probability distribution forecast**
generated by the model, **using the Value-at-Risk risk measure set out in SCR-GP 3.4** (i.e. VaR
of basic own funds at a **99.5% confidence level over a one-year period** — verified in the SCR –
General Provisions Part text *[SCR-GP, stream C]*).

**12.1 — the recalibration allowance.** A firm may use, **for internal modelling purposes**, a
different time period or risk measure than SCR-GP 3.4 **only where** the outputs of the model can
be used by the firm to calculate the SCR "in a manner that provides policyholders with a level of
protection equivalent to that set out in SCR-GP 3.2 to 3.5" (Art. 122(1) SII). So a firm may model
on, say, a multi-year or expected-shortfall basis internally, provided the reported SCR is
equivalent to 99.5% VaR over one year and is derived directly from the probability distribution
forecast under 12.2.

**12.3** — on PRA request, the firm must run its model on **relevant benchmark portfolios using
assumptions based on external rather than internal data**, to verify calibration and check the
specification is in line with generally accepted market practice.

### 17. Profit and loss attribution is deleted; Analysis of Change replaces it [R81]

**Internal Models Chapter 13 "Profit and Loss Attribution" is `[Deleted]`** — rules 13.1, 13.2 and
13.3 are each `[Deleted]`, date-stamped **31/12/2024**, with "past version before 31/12/2024"
markers. The EU Solvency II Article 123 requirement to review, at least annually, the causes and
sources of profits and losses for each major business unit and to show how the risk categorisation
in the model explains them **is no longer a UK rule**.

**Chapter 13A "Analysis of Change" (all rules 31/12/2024) is what replaced it:**
- **13A.1** — a firm with internal model permission must **annually** carry out an analysis
  comparing the change in (1) its SCR at its most recent financial year end and (2) its SCR at its
  previous financial year end.
- **13A.2** — where a first internal model permission takes effect part-way through a financial
  year, compare the SCR at that year end with **the SCR that would have been calculated at the
  previous year end had the permission then been in effect**.
- **13A.3** — the analysis must include **reasons, and documentary evidence supporting those
  reasons**, explaining any change in the SCR.
- **13A.4** — the analysis, reasons and evidence must be **submitted to the PRA as part of the
  information reported under Reporting 2** [R84], starting with the firm's **first financial year
  end on or after 31 December 2025** (or, for a permission taking effect after that date, the
  first year end after it took effect).
- **13A.5** — the 13A.1 duty itself applies for financial years ending on or after **31 December
  2024** (or the date a later permission took effect).

So the obligation is a **year-on-year SCR walk**, not a P&L attribution: it decomposes the change
in the requirement, not the emergence of profit. A model must therefore be able to reproduce the
prior year-end SCR and to step from it to the current one through identifiable, evidenced drivers
(model change, assumption change, data change, new business, market movement, business mix).

### 18. Validation, documentation, and external models and data [R81]

**14.1 — the validation cycle.** A **regular cycle** of internal model validation comprising:
(a) monitoring model performance, reviewing the ongoing appropriateness of the specification and
**testing results against experience**; (b) an **effective statistical process** for validating the
model, enabling the firm to demonstrate to the PRA that the resulting capital requirements are
appropriate; (c) an **analysis of the stability of the model**, in particular testing the
sensitivity of results to changes in key underlying assumptions; and (d) an assessment of the
**accuracy, completeness and appropriateness of the data**. 14.1(2): the statistical methods must
test the appropriateness of the probability distribution forecast against loss experience and all
material new data. 14.1(3): the firm must (a) **compare the coverage of the model with its scope**
and (b) ensure the statistical process includes **stress tests, including a reverse stress test,
identifying the most probable stresses that would threaten the viability of the firm**. 14.1(4):
where a key assumption change has a significant impact on the SCR, the firm must be able to
explain why and how the sensitivity is taken into account in decision-making; **key assumptions
include assumptions on future management actions and assumptions set using expert judgement**.

**14.2 — independence.** The persons or organisational unit carrying out validation must be **free
from influence from those responsible for the development and operation of the model**.

**15.1 / 15.2 — documentation.** Document the design and operational details. The documentation
must (1) demonstrate compliance with Chapters 10–14, 16 and 16A; (2) provide a **detailed outline
of the theory, assumptions, and mathematical and empirical bases** underlying the model; (3)
**indicate any circumstances under which the model does not work effectively**; (4) include all
major changes; and (5) for a partial model, additionally include the justification for the limited
scope, **a description of the integration technique** used to fully integrate the partial model's
capital requirement into the standard formula SCR, and demonstrate compliance with 4.2(2) and (3).

**16.1 / 16.2 — external models and data.** The internal model requirements apply **regardless of
whether** the firm uses a model or data obtained from a third party (Art. 126 SII). The firm must
**monitor any potential limitations** arising from the use of external models or data to ensure
ongoing fulfilment of SCR-GP 3.2–3.5, the internal model requirements, and (for a partial model)
SCR-IM 4.2 and 5.

### 19. Partial internal models: the integration techniques [R81]

**16A.1 — the default.** Unless 16A.2 or 16A.3 applies, the firm must use, as the default
integration technique, **the correlation matrices and formulae of the standard formula** (SCR –
Standard Formula Part [R112]) to fully integrate the partial model's capital requirement into the
standard formula SCR.

**16A.2 / 16A.3 / 16A.4** — where the default would be inappropriate for a 16A.5 reason, the firm
must use the **most appropriate of techniques 1–5 (16B–16G)** and justify its choice; if the
default and all five are inappropriate, it may use an **alternative** technique, justified, and
must ensure it produces an SCR complying with the SCR-GP and Internal Models principles that
**more appropriately reflects the firm's risk profile**.

**16A.5 — when a technique is inappropriate.** Where (1) the resulting SCR (or the firm) would not
comply with SCR-GP 3.2–3.5; (2) the resulting SCR would not appropriately reflect the firm's risk
profile; or (3) the partial model's design is consistent with SCR-GP 3.2–3.5 and 4 but it would
not be possible to use the technique to **fully** integrate its capital requirement into the
standard formula SCR.

**16B.2 — the SCR under techniques 1–5.**
```
SCR = basic SCR (as computed by the chosen technique, 16C-16G)
    + operational risk capital requirement
        (SCR-SF 5 where operational risk is outside the partial model's scope;
         generated by the partial model where it is inside)
    + the adjustment for the loss-absorbing capacity of technical provisions and
      deferred taxes (16B.3 where outside scope; generated by the model where inside)
```
**16B.3** — where the LAC adjustment is outside the partial model's scope it is computed under
SCR-SF 6.1(3), 6.3 and 6.4 [R112] with four changes: the basic SCR is the one from 16C–16G;
SCR-SF 6.3(2)(a)–(d) apply only to standard formula calculations; the capital requirements used
in the basic SCR that are generated by the partial model **must take into account the
risk-mitigating effect of future discretionary benefits**; and the operational risk requirement is
the 16B.2(2) one.

**Technique 1 (16C.1) — simple addition.**
```
BSCR = sum of the capital requirements for the units of the partial internal model
     + the standard formula basic SCR applied only to the risks out of the model's scope
     + SCR_int   (intangible asset risk, SCR-SF 3F1)
```

**Technique 2 (16D) — a bespoke correlation matrix over an aggregation list.**
```
BSCR = sqrt( sum over i,j of Corr(i,j) · SCR_i · SCR_j ) + SCR_int
```
16D.2 — the aggregation list must cover each **unit of the partial internal model**; must include
each standard formula **sub-module** of the non-life (SCR-SF 3.6), life (3.7–3.9), health (3C8.1)
and market (3.11) modules that is **not** within the partial model's scope; and must include the
counterparty default risk module unless it is in scope. Where **none** of a module's sub-modules
are in scope, the list includes the module instead of its sub-modules.
16D.3 — constraints on the correlation parameters: (1) `-1 <= Corr(i,j) <= 1`; (2) symmetry
`Corr(i,j) = Corr(j,i)`; (3) `Corr(i,i) = 1`; (4) **positive semi-definiteness** — for any real
`x_i`, `sum over i,j of Corr(i,j)·x_i·x_j >= 0`; (5) where i and j are standard formula **modules**,
`Corr(i,j)` must equal the standard formula module correlation; (6) where i and j are
**sub-modules of the same module**, `Corr(i,j)` must equal the standard formula sub-module
correlation; (7) `Corr(i,j)` must lie between firm-selected appropriate lower and upper bounds
`Corr_min(i,j)` and `Corr_max(i,j)`. **And then the firm must choose the parameters so that no
other admissible set produces a higher basic SCR** — i.e. a constrained maximisation, not a free
choice.

**Technique 3 (16E) — implied correlations.**
```
BSCR = sqrt( sum over i,j of ( S_S^2 + 2·S_S·( w1·P_C + w2·P_S ) ) + P^2 ) + SCR_int
```
with `S_S` the standard formula basic SCR applied only to risks not covered by the partial model;
`P` the capital requirement for risks within the partial model's scope, generated by the model;
`P_C` the part of `P` for risks within **both** the standard formula's and the partial model's
scope; `P_S` the part of `P` for risks within the partial model but **not** within the standard
formula's scope; and
```
w1 = ( S^2 - S_S^2 - S_C^2 ) / ( d1 + 2·S_S·S_C )                          16E.2
w2 = w1·w3 + 0.5·sqrt( (1 - w1^2)·(1 - w3^2) )                             16E.3
w3 = ( P^2 - P_S^2 - P_C^2 ) / ( d2 + 2·P_S·P_C )                          16E.4
```
where `S` is the standard-formula-style basic SCR with module/sub-module requirements replaced by
the partial model's where possible; `S_C` the standard formula basic SCR restricted to risks
within both scopes, again with module/sub-module requirements replaced by the model's; `d1 = 1` if
`S_S` or `S_C` is zero and `0` otherwise; `d2 = 1` if `P_S` or `P_C` is zero and `0` otherwise.
(The formula as printed in 16D/16E carries a `sum over i,j` operator whose index set is not
defined in the rule text for 16E — recorded in Gaps as printed, not reinterpreted.)

**Technique 4 (16F) — module-by-module cross terms.**
```
BSCR = sqrt( P^2 + S_S^2
             + sum over j = k+1..n of 2·S_j · ( sum over i = 1..l of Corr(i,j)·P_i
                                              + sum over i = l+1..k of Corr(i,j)·S_i ) )
     + SCR_int
```
`k` = number of standard formula modules within the partial model's scope; `n` = number of
standard formula modules; `l` = number of modules for which the partial model can generate the
capital requirement; `Corr(i,j)` = the standard formula module correlation; `P_i` = the module i
requirement generated by the partial model; `S_i`, `S_j` = module requirements generated by the
standard formula, or, where the module has sub-modules, by applying the same formula one level
down (16F.2), and one level down again for nested sub-modules (16F.3, date-stamped 24/07/2025),
with `SCR_int` set to zero at those inner levels.

**Technique 5 (16G) — scaled cross terms.**
```
BSCR = sqrt( P^2 + S_S^2
             + ( 2·P / sqrt( sum over i,j = 1..k of Corr(i,j)·S_i·S_j ) )
               · sum over j = k+1..n of sum over i = 1..k of Corr(i,j)·S_i·S_j )
     + SCR_int
```
with `P`, `S_S`, `k`, `n`, `Corr(i,j)` and `SCR_int` as in 16F.1, and the same recursive treatment
of modules with sub-modules (16G.2, 16G.3).

### 20. PRA expectations on internal models: SS1/24 and model drift [R81b][R81c]

**SS1/24 (effective 31 December 2024; "internal model requirements" = SCR-IM 10 to 16A).**
- **para 2.1** — the probability distribution forecast of a **partial** model is expected to be
  calculated **at the highest level of aggregation of the partial model's components**; where the
  partial model consists of different components that are separately calculated and not aggregated
  within it, the PDF is expected to be calculated **for each component**.
- **paras 2.2–2.3** — **including a new risk in the model is expected to be a major model change**.
  Two exceptions may be justified as minor: (a) splitting or combining risks already within scope,
  and (b) an additional risk that falls within a sub-module of a risk already within scope. The
  justification must be in the model change records.
- **paras 2.4–2.6 — operational definitions of the 11.4 data tests.** *Accurate*: free from
  material errors; where unavoidable data limitations exist, consider the impact on model
  credibility and make appropriate adjustments, justifying expert judgement. *Complete*: the data
  include **sufficient historical information to assess the characteristics of the underlying risk,
  in particular to identify trends**. *Appropriate*: the amount and nature of the data ensure the
  estimations made on the basis of the data **do not include a material estimation error**.
- **para 2.7** — the validation process is expected to apply to **all parts** of the model and to
  cover SCR-GP 3.2–3.5 and SCR-IM 3.2, 10 to 13A, and 15 to 16A (plus 4.2 and 5.1 for a partial
  model application).
- **paras 2.8–2.9** — firms are expected to specify the processes and methods used to validate and
  their purposes, and **who is responsible for each validation task**; and to assess the quality
  and independence of validation, considering reporting structure (internal validation) or
  remuneration structure and other mandates (external validation).
- **para 2.10 — the annual attestation.** The PRA expects an appropriate Senior Management Function
  holder, **"in most cases the Chief Risk Officer (SMF4)"**, to attest annually in writing either
  that the firm satisfies SCR-GP 3.3–3.4 and the internal model requirements (subject to
  modifications and any additional PRA requirements), or that it satisfies all the others and has
  **a credible plan** to address non-compliance identified by validation.
- **paras 2.11–2.12 — validation tools.** The statistical validation process is expected to be
  based on current information (including developments in actuarial techniques and generally
  accepted market practice) and on a detailed understanding of the economic and actuarial theory
  and assumptions underlying the PDF methods; and to include **an analysis of the stability of the
  model's outputs for different calculations using the same input data** (i.e. Monte Carlo noise).
- **paras 2.13–2.14 — the documentation standard.** Documentation must be sufficient for **any
  independent knowledgeable third party** to understand the design and operational details and
  form a sound judgement on compliance; and the PRA expects firms to be able to **reproduce
  outputs of the model using the documentation and all of the inputs**.
- **para 2.15 — minimum documentation content**, fourteen items: (a) an inventory of the documents;
  (b) the model change policy; (c) policies, controls and procedures for managing the model,
  including staff responsibilities; (d) the IT used, including contingency plans; (e) all relevant
  assumptions and their justification; (f) the methodology for setting assumptions — the inputs on
  which the choice is based, the objectives and criteria for appropriateness, and **any limitations
  in the choice**; (g) **a directory of the data used, specifying source, characteristics and
  usage**; (h) where data are not used consistently over time, a description of the inconsistency
  and its justification; (i) the qualitative and quantitative coverage indicators under 11.6A and
  11.6B; (j) a description of the risk-mitigation techniques recognised and how the risks arising
  from their use are reflected; (k) **a description of the future management actions taken into
  account** under 11.8; (l) the validation specifications from para 2.8; (m) the validation results
  on compliance with SCR-GP 3.2–3.5; and (n) for external models and data, their role, the reasons
  for preferring them to internal alternatives, and the alternatives considered.
- **para 2.16 — circumstances under which the model does not work effectively** (the 15.2(3) item):
  risks not covered; limitations in the risk modelling used; the nature, degree and sources of
  uncertainty in the results including sensitivity to key assumptions; deficiencies in data and
  lack of data; risks from external models and data; IT limitations; and governance limitations.
- **para 2.17 — model change records** must include the rationale for major and minor changes, the
  implications of major changes for design and operations, and, where a major change or a
  combination of minor changes has a material impact on outputs, **a quantitative and qualitative
  comparison of outputs before and after the change at the same valuation date**.

**SS15/16 — model drift and standard formula SCR reporting.**
- **para 2.1** — model drift is "the risk that capital requirements calculated using an internal
  model may, over time, become less reflective of the risks to which firms are exposed".
- **para 2.2 / 2.3** — supervisory tools include monitoring the internal model SCR against
  alternative measures, analysis of model outputs, and analysis of **quarterly model change
  reporting**. The alternative measures include **the standard formula SCR, the pre-corridor MCR,
  net written premium and best estimate liabilities** — note "pre-corridor MCR", i.e. MCR_linear
  before the 25%/45% collar in MCR 3.1B [R78].
- **para 2.3A / 2.3B** — different tools may be used for different sub-sectors, in particular
  different tools for life and general insurance firms, and the approach may evolve.
- **paras 3.3–3.5** — SCR-IM 3.4 lets the PRA request a standard formula SCR estimate; the PRA can
  revoke model permission, in which case the firm would have to use the standard formula; so firms
  with model permission are expected to **maintain the ability to calculate their SCR using the
  standard formula**.
- **paras 3.5A–3.8, 3.11** — the *submission* expectation is narrower: insurers (including
  composites) **with material non-life technical provisions** that use an internal model for the
  solo SCR are expected to report their **non-life** standard formula SCR privately in full via
  **XBRL**, not externally audited, approved by a suitably authorised senior manager, **four weeks
  after** the firm's annual QRT submission date under the Reporting Part [R84], through **BEEDS**
  as an occasional submission. Group-level requests are made via the supervisor (para 3.9).
  **A life-only internal model firm is not caught by the submission expectation** but is caught by
  the maintain-the-capability expectation in para 3.5.

**Notes on every non-obvious mark.**

- **Surplus funds — `(x)` for WOL and PA, `--` for TA/CI/IP/ULB.** The Surplus Funds Part applies
  to a firm carrying on **with-profits insurance business** and computes surplus funds **per
  with-profits fund** [R79 1.1, 3.1]. A with-profits whole-of-life or a with-profits deferred
  annuity written inside that fund is inside the calculation; the same product written as a
  non-profit contract is not. Non-profit business written *inside* a with-profits fund is excluded
  from *with-profits assets* by the 1.2 definition, so it neither adds to nor subtracts from
  surplus funds.
- **Ancillary own funds — `(x)` everywhere.** AOF is an entity capital-structure item, not a
  product item; nothing in a liability projection produces it. Marked conditional rather than
  applicable because it exists only where the firm holds an AOF permission [R77 2.5].
- **EPIFP — `--` for every product.** Not a "does not apply because of product characteristics"
  mark: the requirement itself has been removed from all Solvency UK reporting and disclosure
  [R86 paras 4.43–4.44] and no EPIFP rule exists in the Own Funds Part [R77]. See Extracted
  mechanics §8.
- **MCR TP_l1 / TP_l2 — `(x)` for WOL and PA.** The terms turn on "long-term insurance obligations
  **with profit participation**", not on the product name. A with-profits whole-of-life splits
  across TP_l1 (guaranteed benefits) and TP_l2 (FDB); a non-profit whole-of-life falls entirely in
  TP_l4. A with-profits or bonus-participating annuity likewise splits; a level non-profit
  pension annuity is TP_l4.
- **MCR TP_l3 — `(x)` for WOL and WP.** "Linked long-term liabilities" catches a unit-linked
  whole-of-life and the unit-linked element of a unitised with-profits or linked-plus-with-profits
  structure; the pure with-profits element is TP_l1/TP_l2.
- **MCR capital at risk — `(x)` for IP, WP, ULB, PA.** CAR is defined by reference to what the firm
  would pay on **death or disability** [R78 3C.1(5)(a)]. TA, CI and WOL carry it obviously. IP is
  marked conditional because the "disability" leg is engaged but the amount the firm "would
  currently pay in the event of … disability" for a benefit paid as an income stream is an EPV
  computation rather than a sum assured, and the rule does not spell that out. ULB and WP carry it
  only through their death benefit in excess of the fund/asset share. PA carries it only where the
  annuity has a death benefit (guarantee period, value protection, spouse's reversion) — the sign
  is typically negative and the per-contract floor at zero then bites.
- **MCR general insurance segments — `(x)` for IP only.** MCR 6.1 segment 2 is "Income protection
  insurance", lines of business 2 and 14, with alpha = 13.1% and beta = 8.5% [R78]. That segment
  applies only where IP is written as **general insurance** class 2/14, not as long-term business.
  This library treats IP as long-term business, so the mark is conditional on the writing basis.
- **Ring-fenced fund — `(x)` for WOL.** The PRA expects each with-profits fund to be an RFF
  [R80b para 2.2]. A with-profits whole-of-life sits inside one; a non-profit whole-of-life does
  not. TA, CI, IP, ULB and PA written in the shareholder fund are outside the ring fence. The
  glossary definition is generic ("an identifiable unit of assets and liabilities where …
  restrictions … give rise to restricted own funds"), so other arrangements could qualify — the
  retrieved sources name only with-profits funds and their COBS 20 sub-funds.
- **MA portfolio — `x` for PA, `(x)` for IP and WP.** An MA portfolio is expressly **not** an RFF
  [R80 glossary] but attracts the identical Own Funds 3L notional-SCR deduction and SCR-SF 9
  no-diversification treatment. PA is the archetypal MA business [R2]; IP in-payment claims and
  the guaranteed element of a with-profits annuity can enter an MA portfolio through the
  "eligible element" route recorded at [R2].
- **Notional SCR / no diversification — `(x)` for IP and WOL.** Follows the RFF and MA-portfolio
  marks above: it bites wherever the product sits in a with-profits fund or an MA portfolio, not
  because of the product's own risk profile.
- **RFF profit-participation adjustment — `(x)` for WOL.** SCR-SF 9.1(5) applies "where profit
  participation arrangements exist in respect of any insurance and reinsurance obligations within a
  ring-fenced fund", i.e. a with-profits whole-of-life inside a with-profits fund, not a non-profit
  one.
- **Prospective valuation route — `x` for WOL.** SS13/15 para 3.1 names **whole-of-life policies**
  as the example where the retrospective calculation may be negative or significantly lower than
  the prospective value and the prospective basis may therefore be necessary [R79b]. `(x)` for PA
  because a with-profits deferred annuity in payment has the same characteristic but is not named.
- **IM options and guarantees — `x` for WP and ULB, `(x)` elsewhere.** SCR-IM 11.7 applies to every
  product with a material option or guarantee; WP (bonus, smoothing, guaranteed surrender/maturity,
  GAOs) and ULB (guarantees on a linked bond, plus policyholder switching and surrender options)
  are where it materially binds. TA/CI/IP/WOL/PA carry conditional marks for renewability,
  reviewability, and annuity commutation/value-protection options.
- **IM future management actions — `x` for WP and ULB.** These are the two products where
  discretion (bonus and smoothing policy; charge levels and fund choices) is a modelled management
  action rather than a marginal one.
- **Partial model integration techniques — `(x)` everywhere.** They apply at entity level to a firm
  with a partial internal model permission, not to a product. Marked conditional throughout.
- **Standard formula SCR XBRL submission — `(x)` for IP only, `--` otherwise.** SS15/16 para 3.6
  expects submission only from insurers **with material non-life technical provisions** [R81c].
  A life-only writer of any of these seven products is outside it. IP is marked conditional
  because IP written as general insurance class 2 would create non-life technical provisions.

## Gaps and caveats

### Conflicts between retrieved sources — recorded, not resolved

1. **Own Funds Chapter 4 versus Chapter 4A.** Both are live in the 05/08/2026 view. Chapter 4
   (rules dated 01/01/2016, noting Art. 98 SII) states the limits as fractions of **eligible own
   funds**: Tier 1 more than one-third and Tier 3 less than one-third for the SCR; Tier 1 more
   than 50% for the MCR. Chapter 4A (rules dated 31/12/2024) states them as percentages of the
   **SCR** and the **MCR**: Tier 1 at least 50% of the SCR, Tier 3 less than 15% of the SCR,
   Tier 2 + Tier 3 at most 50% of the SCR; Tier 1 at least 80% of the MCR, Tier 2 at most 20% of
   the MCR. These are different tests on different denominators. SS2/15 para 1.3(d) [R83] says
   "**For the purposes of Own Funds 4, Own Funds 4A sets out the applicable limits**", which reads
   as treating 4A as operative and 4 as the enabling provision, but **no retrieved rule says
   Chapter 4 is disapplied**. Documented as-is.
2. **MCR 3.1B versus MCR 3.3.** 3.1B (31/12/2024) gives the corridor arithmetically as
   `min(max(MCR_linear, 0.25·SCR), 0.45·SCR)` and is silent on capital add-ons. 3.3 (01/01/2016)
   restates the corridor in words and adds "**including any capital add-on which has been
   imposed**". A drafter must apply the add-on inclusion from 3.3; the formula in 3.1B does not
   say so on its face.
3. **The Own Funds 3C / 3L interaction is admitted by the PRA to be unclear.** CP4/26 Proposal 3
   [R83c] proposes "**Removing unnecessary text and clarifying the interaction of Own Funds 3C and
   Own Funds 3L** when determining the amount of restricted own funds to deduct from the excess of
   assets over liabilities when calculating the reconciliation reserve", and separately proposes
   to correct an inconsistency that "would cancel out the increase in eligible own funds following
   receipt of a classification of own funds permission for an item recognised as a liability on
   the Solvency II balance sheet". **PS18/26 [R83d] finalised only Proposal 1**, so as at
   2026-08-06 both defects remain in the live rule text. Any downstream document describing the
   reconciliation-reserve deduction must say so.
4. **EIOPA RFF Guideline 11 ¶1.25 goes further than the PRA rules.** It states the effect of the
   adjustment is to allow only own funds equal to the notional SCR to contribute to coverage of
   the SCR **and to the coverage of the MCR** [R80c]. Own Funds 3L operates textually on the
   *reconciliation reserve*, and no retrieved PRA rule says the RFF deduction bites the MCR
   coverage test specifically. Consistent, but only the guideline is explicit.
5. **SCR-IM 8.1 versus SCR-IM 3.4 and SS15/16.** 8.1 forbids reverting to the standard formula for
   any part of the SCR; 3.4 requires the firm to be able to produce a standard formula estimate on
   request, and SS15/16 para 3.5 [R81c] expects the capability to be maintained. Not a
   contradiction, but it does mean an internal model firm must **operate two complete calculation
   paths over the same liability cash flows**, only one of which may be used for the SCR.
6. **The printed formula in Internal Models 16E.1 carries an undefined index.** As retrieved, it
   reads `BSCR = sqrt( sum over i,j of ( S_S^2 + 2·S_S·(w1·P_C + w2·P_S) ) + P^2 ) + SCR_int`.
   No aggregation list or index set is defined for `i,j` in 16E, unlike 16D.2. Transcribed exactly
   as printed and **not reinterpreted**. A drafter using technique 3 must go back to the rule.

### Questions the retrieved sources do not settle

1. **What "surplus funds" means as a defined term** — see fetch failure 1. The Surplus Funds Part
   tells you how to *calculate* the amount but never defines the concept.
2. **Whether an MCR breach can lead to withdrawal of authorisation.** The Undertakings in
   Difficulty Part [R82] contains no such rule, and no other retrieved document supplies one. In
   EU Solvency II this consequence sits in Art. 144; the UK equivalent, if any, sits outside the
   Rulebook Part read here and was **not located**. Do not assert it.
3. **Whether "foreseeable dividends, distributions and charges" (Own Funds 3C.1(2)) is defined
   anywhere.** It is not defined in the Own Funds Part. Whether the reconciliation reserve must be
   reduced by a board-approved dividend, a dividend consistent with a stated policy, or something
   else, is unresolved.
4. **How a firm selects the "scenario under which the basic own funds of the firm as a whole are
   most negatively affected" (SCR-SF 9.1(6)–(7)) in practice** when the sub-module stresses are
   bidirectional and the RFF FDB adjustment is applied per fund. The rule prescribes the summation
   but not a search procedure. EIOPA Guideline 9(d) [R80c] adds only the floor-at-zero rule for a
   negative worst-case charge.
5. **Whether Own Funds 3L.1's internal-model notional SCR inherits the SCR-SF 9.1(6)–(7)
   firm-worst-scenario selection.** 3L.1 says only "using that internal model, as if the firm
   pursued only the business included in the ring-fenced fund or matching adjustment portfolio".
   EIOPA Guideline 10 [R80c] requires consistency with the whole-firm calculation but does not
   settle the scenario-selection point.
6. **Whether the RFF own-funds deduction reduces MCR-eligible own funds.** See conflict 4.
7. **Whether the SS1/24 para 2.10 annual CRO attestation must be submitted to the PRA or merely
   held.** SS1/24 [R81b] says only that the PRA "expects an appropriate individual in a Senior
   Management Function role … to provide on an annual basis a written attestation"; it does not say
   to whom, or by what channel.
8. **How income protection's "disability" benefit enters the MCR capital-at-risk term.** MCR
   3C.1(5) is drafted around an amount "the firm would currently pay in the event of the death or
   disability of the persons insured". For an income benefit that is an EPV, not a sum assured,
   and the rule does not say how to strike it. Marked `(x)` for IP in the applicability matrix for
   this reason.
9. **Whether the Own Funds Part's "own funds lists" is a defined term with a fixed content beyond
   Chapters 3A, 3D, 3F, 3H and 3J.** The term is used in 3.4 and 5.1 and is italicised as a
   glossary term in the rulebook rendering, but the definition was not retrieved (glossary letter
   O not fetched).
10. **How the MCR interacts with ring-fenced funds at all.** MCR 3C.1 aggregates technical
    provisions across the firm with no RFF split, and the MCR Part contains no RFF rule. Whether
    a with-profits fund's technical provisions enter MCR_linear undiminished is not addressed by
    any retrieved source.
