## Scope and applicability

### What business each item governs

| Item | Business governed (paraphrase, with pinpoint) |
|---|---|
| **A-585** | Universal life insurance policies. ¶7 (p. 1102) defines one as a life insurance policy to which "separately identified interest credits" and mortality and expense charges are made — interest credited through dividend accumulations, premium deposit funds or other supplementary accounts does not count. Both **flexible premium** (¶3) and **fixed premium** (¶2) forms are in scope, and **interest-indexed** UL is defined at ¶4 but see the gaps section. The reserve is a terminal reserve at each policy anniversary for **the basic policy plus any benefits or riders "for which premiums are not paid separately"** (¶8, p. 1102) — separately-premium-rated riders are outside it. |
| **A-250** | "Variable annuity" (¶1, p. 1095): an individual or group policy or contract whose annuity benefits vary with the investment experience of one or more separate accounts the insurer maintains for it. |
| **A-255** | "Modified Guaranteed Annuity" (¶1, p. 1096): an individual or group **deferred annuity** whose underlying assets sit in a **separate account** and whose values are **guaranteed if held for specified periods**; it carries "nonforfeiture values that are based upon a market-value adjustment formula if held for shorter periods". That formula may, but need not, reflect the separate account's asset values, and the assets must stay in a separate account for as long as the holder can surrender. |
| **A-270** | "Variable life insurance policy" (¶1, p. 1097): an individual or group policy providing life insurance whose amount or duration varies with the investment experience of one or more separate accounts the insurer establishes and maintains for it. |

### Effective dates — there are none

**This is the single most important applicability finding, and it is a negative one.** Searched
across all nine extracted pages: **none of A-585, A-250, A-255 or A-270 prints an effective date, an
operative date, an applicability threshold, a size test, a transition provision or any grandfathering
language.** The words "effective", "operative", "transition", "applies to policies issued on or
after", "grandfather" and any date other than the copyright years do not appear.

There is exactly **one** issue-year keying rule in the four items, and it is an *assumption-selection*
rule rather than an applicability rule — A-585 ¶8.j (p. 1104). It requires every present value to be
computed on (i) the interest rate or rates and (ii) the mortality rates that Appendix A-820 sets "for
policies issued in the same year", together with (iii) whatever other tables are needed to value
supplementary benefits under a rider that is valued with the policy. ¶10 (p. 1104) reinforces it: the
mortality and interest bases for those present values are A-820's minimum standards.

**Consequence for the library.** Any statement of the form "A-585 applies to issues before/after
*date*" cannot be sourced to A-585. Its temporal reach is set entirely outside it — by the Standard
Valuation Law's year-of-issue minimum-standard machinery, by SVL §11 and the Valuation Manual
operative date, and by VM-20 §3.B.6's routing of the *All Other* NPR to VM-A/VM-C. A-585 itself
simply states a method and delegates its parameters to A-820 by year of issue. **Do not infer dates
from this text.**

## Mechanics

### A-585 — definitions that carry weight (¶¶1–7, p. 1102)

Paraphrased, keeping each defined term; the ones that change a calculation are marked ●.

1. ● **"Cash surrender value"** — the net cash surrender value with any outstanding policy loans added
   back. (So "cash surrender value" in this item is *gross of* loans; "net cash surrender value" is the
   payable amount.)
2. **"Fixed premium universal life insurance policy"** — any UL policy that is not a flexible premium
   UL policy.
3. ● **"Flexible premium universal life insurance policy"** — a UL policy under which the policyowner
   may change, each independently of the others, how much or when one or more premiums are paid, or
   the amount of insurance. (This definition is what switches the r-ratio on — see ¶8.d.)
4. **"Interest-indexed universal life insurance policy"** — any UL policy whose interest credits are
   "linked to an external referent".
5. **"Net cash surrender value"** — the most the policyowner can be paid on surrender.
6. ● **"Policy value"** — the amount against which a UL policy's separately identified interest credits
   and its mortality, expense or other charges are made. (The quantity compared to the Guaranteed
   Maturity Fund in ¶8.d and projected in ¶8.i.)
7. ● **"Universal life insurance policy"** — paraphrased in the scope table above. Its closing sentence
   lets a UL policy carry other credits and charges as well, giving charges for the cost of rider
   benefits as the example.

### A-585 — the reserve construction (¶8, pp. 1102–1104)

The standard is stated first (¶8, p. 1102). The minimum valuation standard for UL is the
**Commissioners Reserve Valuation Method** in the form the item goes on to describe for these
policies, on the tables and interest rates it specifies. The terminal reserve at any policy
anniversary — for the basic policy together with any benefits or riders that carry no separate
premium — equals the **net level premium reserve less (C) and less (D)**, each term defined in the
sub-paragraphs that follow.

So, with `t` the duration and `x` the issue age:

```
V(t)  =  NetLevelPremiumReserve(t)  −  (C)  −  (D)                                        [¶8]

NetLevelPremiumReserve(t) = ( (A) − (B) ) · r                                             [¶8.a]

(A) = present value of all future guaranteed benefits at the date of valuation            [¶8.a.i]

(B) = ( PVFB / ä_x ) · ä_{x+t}                                                            [¶8.a.ii]

(C) = ( (a) − (b) ) · ( ä_{x+t} / ä_x ) · r                                               [¶8.f]

(D) = Σ additional quantities analogous to (C) arising from structural changes            [¶8.g]
```

**Symbols, each defined by the text itself (paraphrased):**

- **PVFB** (¶8.a.ii, p. 1103) — the present value of all benefits guaranteed at issue, on the
  assumption that the policyowner pays the future guaranteed maturity premiums, allowing for every
  guarantee in the policy or declared by the insurer. Note it is fixed *at issue*, in contrast to
  (A), which ¶8.a.i measures "at the date of valuation". `PVFB / ä_x` is therefore the net level
  valuation premium, and (A) − (B) is the ordinary prospective net level premium reserve.
- **ä_x, ä_{x+t}** (¶8.b, p. 1103) — present values of an annuity of one per year, paid on each policy
  anniversary from ages x and x+t respectively and running to the policy's **"highest attained age
  at which a premium may be paid"**; x is the issue age and t the policy duration.
  **The annuity term is the premium-paying period, not the coverage period** — an easy implementation
  error.
- **r** (¶8.d, p. 1103) — one, except for a **flexible premium** policy whose policy value is below the
  guaranteed maturity fund; for that policy r is the policy value divided by the guaranteed maturity
  fund. i.e.

  ```
  r = 1                                       if the policy is a FIXED premium UL policy
  r = min( 1 , PolicyValue(t) / GMF(t) )      if the policy is a FLEXIBLE premium UL policy
  ```

  **The fixed-premium branch is unconditional: r is always 1 there.** A blanket
  `r = min(1, PV/GMF)` is wrong for fixed premium UL.
- **(a) − (b)** (¶8.f, p. 1103) — defined by reference to **"paragraph 9 of Appendix A-820"**,
  applied to the plan of insurance that the guaranteed maturity premiums and all guarantees in the
  policy or declared by the insurer define at issue. **A-820 was not extracted for this work; the
  content of (a) and (b) is therefore not stated here.** Structurally it is the CRVM expense
  allowance, applied to a synthetic plan of insurance whose premiums are the GMPs.

**Guaranteed Maturity Premium (¶8.c, p. 1103).** Three sentences, paraphrased:

- **Flexible premium UL.** The GMP is the level gross premium — payable at issue and periodically
  after that, for as long as the policy allows premiums to be paid — that matures the policy, for an
  amount consistent with the policy structure (footnote 1), on the latest maturity date the policy
  permits or, where it sets none, at the highest age of the valuation mortality table.
- **Basis.** The GMP is calculated at issue on all policy guarantees at issue "excluding guarantees
  linked to an external referent".
- **Fixed premium UL.** The GMP is the premium the policy defines that, at issue, provides the minimum
  policy guarantees (footnote 2).

The two footnotes are operative, not commentary:

- **Footnote 1 (p. 1103) — the maturity amount.** Where the death benefit is level for the life of the
  policy, the maturity amount is the **initial death benefit**; where the death benefit is a specified
  amount plus the policy value or cash surrender value, it is the **specified amount**. In both cases
  the existence of a minimum-death-benefit corridor is disregarded.
- **Footnote 2 (p. 1103) — corridors and underfunding.** For both flexible and fixed premium policies
  the GMP **must be adjusted for any death benefit corridors** the policy provides. The GMP **may be
  lower than the premium needed to cover all charges**; the footnote says this is especially likely in
  the first year of a policy with heavy first-year expense charges.

So the GMP is a **solve**: the level gross premium, payable over the allowed premium-paying period,
that drives the policy value to the maturity amount at the latest permitted maturity date (or the
end of the valuation mortality table if none), computed on guarantees at issue, **with guarantees
linked to an external referent excluded**, and **adjusted for death benefit corridors**. The maturity
amount is the initial death benefit for an Option A / level design and the specified amount for an
Option B / specified-amount-plus-value design, in both cases ignoring the corridor.

**Guaranteed Maturity Fund (¶8.e, p. 1103).** At each duration the GMF is the fund which, combined with
the guaranteed maturity premiums still to come, would carry the policy to maturity on the guarantees
in force at issue.

The GMF is thus the prospective guarantee-basis fund path implied by the GMP — a second solve, per
duration.

**Projection rule for future guaranteed benefits (¶8.i, p. 1104).** Two steps: (1) project **whichever
is larger of the GMF and the policy value**, allowing for any future guaranteed maturity premiums and
applying every guarantee of interest, mortality and expense deduction (and similar) found in the policy
or declared by the insurer; then (2) bring in any benefits guaranteed by the policy or by declaration
that do not depend on the policy value.

**This is the mechanism the r-ratio pairs with, and the pairing is the design logic of the whole
item.** When a flexible-premium policy is underfunded (policy value < GMF), the benefit projection
in (A) uses the **greater** of the two — i.e. the GMF, giving a *fully funded* reserve — and the
resulting net level premium reserve is then **scaled down by r = policy value / GMF**. Over-funded
policies project on the policy value itself and take r = 1.

**Declared guarantees (¶9, p. 1104).** Where the insurer declares guarantees more favourable than the
policy's contractual guarantees, future guaranteed benefits are determined on the declared guarantees.

**Structural changes (¶¶8.g, 8.h, 11 and footnote 3).** ¶8.h (p. 1104) requires the GMP, the GMF and
(B) to be **recalculated for any structural change** in the policy, consistently with the definitions
above. ¶8.g (p. 1104) defines (D) as the sum of any further quantities **analogous to (C)** that
structural changes (footnote 3) create, each computed on a basis consistent with (C) but using the
maturity date that applied when the change was made.

Footnote 3 (p. 1104) is the only place the scope of "structural change" is fixed. Its three rules:

1. **General definition.** Structural changes are changes that stand apart from the automatic workings
   of the policy. They are usually initiated by the policyholder and include changes to the guaranteed
   benefits, to the latest maturity date or to the allowable premium payment period.
2. **Fixed premium UL with at-most-annual redetermination.** Where all credits and charges of a fixed
   premium UL policy are redetermined no more often than annually, on policy anniversaries, the term
   also covers any change to guaranteed benefits or to the fixed premiums that the GMP did not
   anticipate at issue — even when it results from the policy's automatic workings.
3. **Recomputing (B) for such a change.** When (B) under ¶8.a.ii is recomputed for a fixed premium UL
   structural change, PVFB must leave out the guaranteed benefits that are funded by the excess of the
   insurer's declared interest, mortality and expense guarantees over the policy's guarantees at issue.

¶11 (p. 1104) gives the sanctioned simplification, and is the closest thing in the item to a worked
method. It says that consistent methods are prescribed for reserves when structural changes are made,
that several are possible, and that the simplest may be to keep the GMF and GMP values **proportional
to the current face amount** — compute them per dollar of face and multiply by the new face amount —
which, the paragraph says, avoids much of the complexity of the other methods.

**Implementation reading:** hold GMP and GMF **per $1 of face**, and rescale on a face change. That
turns (D) from an open-ended accumulation of change layers into a rescaling, at the cost of being
only one permitted method ("several such methods are possible") — it is permissive language, not a
prescription.

### A-585 — Alternative Minimum Reserves (¶¶12–13, pp. 1104–1105)

The trigger and the test (¶12, pp. 1104–1105), paraphrased:

- **Trigger (p. 1104).** The test applies if, in any policy year, a UL policy's GMP is **less than its
  valuation net premium** — that valuation net premium being computed by the valuation method actually
  used for the reserve, but on the **minimum valuation standards** of mortality and interest. The
  minimum reserve is then the greater of legs a. and b.
- **Leg a. (p. 1105).** The reserve on the method, mortality table and interest rate actually used.
- **Leg b. (p. 1105).** The reserve on the method actually used, but on minimum-standard mortality and
  interest, with the **GMP substituted for the valuation net premium** in each policy year in which the
  valuation net premium exceeds the GMP.

And the valuation net premium the test runs on (¶13, p. 1105), in two forms. On a **net level premium
basis**:

```
        PVFB
        ────
         ä_x
```

On a **Commissioners Reserve Valuation Method** basis:

```
        PVFB     (a) − (b)
        ──── +   ─────────
         ä_x        ä_x
```

**As a procedure:**

```
for each policy year k:
    VNP(k) = valuation net premium on the method ACTUALLY used, on MINIMUM mortality and interest
             ( = PVFB/ä_x                        on a net level premium basis          [¶13]
               = PVFB/ä_x + ((a)−(b))/ä_x        on a CRVM basis                       [¶13] )
    if GMP < VNP(k) for any k:
        AMR_b = reserve on the method actually used, on MINIMUM mortality and interest,
                with VNP(k) REPLACED BY GMP in every year k where VNP(k) > GMP
        MinimumReserve = max( reserve on the method/table/rate actually used , AMR_b )
```

Three details that decide an implementation:

- The trigger is **per policy year** ("If, in any policy year …"), but the replacement in (b) is also
  applied **year by year** — only "in each policy year" in which the valuation net premium exceeds the
  GMP — so a policy can be in the test with the substitution active in only some years.
- Both legs are computed on the **method actually used**; only the mortality/interest basis is forced
  to the minimum standards in leg (b) and in the trigger test.
- The GMP replaces the *valuation net premium*, not the gross premium. This is structurally the same
  shape as a deficiency test but its comparator is the **Guaranteed Maturity Premium**, not the
  contract gross premium.

**No worked example, no tables and no factors appear anywhere in A-585.** All mortality and interest
parameters are delegated to Appendix A-820 (¶¶8.j, 10). The item prints no numbers at all beyond
paragraph numbers.

### A-250 — Variable Annuities (PDF p. 1095)

The whole item is seven printed lines of substance. Definitions:

- ¶1 "Variable annuity" (paraphrased in the scope table above).

Then two operative paragraphs, which are the entire valuation content (paraphrased):

- **¶2.** In each such separate account the company must hold assets worth at least the reserves and

- **¶3.** The variable annuity reserve liability is set under the requirements of Appendix A-820, by

and (ii) any mortality guarantees (¶3). It is not even given its own item heading for "Valuation
Requirements" — the three paragraphs sit under "Definitions".

### A-255 — Modified Guaranteed Annuities (PDF p. 1096)

Definitions: ¶1 "Modified Guaranteed Annuity" (paraphrased in the scope table above); ¶2 **"Interest
credits"** — every amount of interest credited to the contract; ¶3 **"Separate account"** — one
established under the insurance laws that apply to the insurer.

**Valuation Requirements** (¶¶4–6, p. 1096), paraphrased — again this is the entire content:

- **¶4.** MGA reserve liabilities are set as Appendix A-820 provides, by actuarial procedures that
  recognise (a) that the separate account's assets are based on market values, (b) the variable
  nature of the benefits and (c) any mortality guarantees.
- **¶5.** At minimum, the separate account liability equals the surrender value produced by **the
  market-value adjustment formula in the contract**. If that liability exceeds the market value of the
  assets, assets are transferred into the separate account until their market value at least equals
  the liabilities. Any further reserve needed for future guaranteed benefits must also be set up.
- **¶6.** The MVA formula, the interest guarantees and how closely projected asset and liability cash
  flows are matched must also be considered, and the company must decide whether the separate
  account's assets are adequate for every guaranteed future benefit.

**Separate Accounts** (¶7, p. 1096): in each separate account the insurer must hold assets worth at
least the valuation reserves and other contract liabilities for that account.

**Finding: A-255 is a pointer plus one floor.** It is thicker than A-250 by exactly three operative
rules, and they are worth having:

1. A **floor**: separate account liability ≥ the surrender value produced by **the contract's own
   MVA formula** (¶5). The MVA formula is the contract's, not a prescribed one — A-255 prints no MVA
   formula and no parameters for one.
2. An **asset top-up**: if that liability exceeds the market value of the separate account assets, a
   transfer into the separate account must make market value at least equal the liability (¶5). This
   is a general-account-funds-the-shortfall rule stated on the asset side.
3. A **residual reserve**: whatever further reserve future guaranteed benefits need must be established
   (¶5), with ¶6 requiring consideration of the MVA formula, the interest guarantees and asset/liability
   cash flow matching, and an affirmative company determination of asset adequacy for all guaranteed
   benefits.

Everything else — the reserve method itself — is delegated to **Appendix A-820**. **A-255 does not
mention CARVM, does not prescribe an interim value, and does not contain a single formula, symbol,
table or factor.**

**A-255 ¶1 is also a scope definition with load-bearing use elsewhere.** The library records that
VM-21 §2.A.2 excludes contracts falling under VM-A item A-255 [REG-R35, recorded at
`_research/registered-index-linked-annuity.md` and `_research/variable-annuity.md`]. The test
for that exclusion is the ¶1 definition paraphrased above; its four elements are (i) deferred annuity,
individual or group, (ii) underlying assets held in a separate account, (iii) values guaranteed if
held for specified periods, (iv) nonforfeiture values based on an MVA formula if held for shorter
periods — plus a separate-account location requirement for the underlying assets that runs "during the
period or periods when the contract holder can surrender the contract" (¶1, p. 1096). The exclusion
itself is VM-21's text, not A-255's; A-255 supplies only the definition.

### A-270 — Variable Life Insurance (PDF pp. 1097–1099) — *no reference id assigned*

Fourteen definitions (¶¶1–14, pp. 1097–1098). The ones that feed a calculation, paraphrased:

- ¶3 **"Assumed investment rate"** — the investment return a variable life policy would need to be
  credited, net of charges for taxes, investment expenses and mortality and expense guarantees, for its
  variable death benefit to stay equal at all times to the death benefit (excluding incidental
  insurance benefits) that the plan of insurance would pay if the death benefit did not move with the
  separate account's investment experience.
- ¶4 **"Benefit base"** — the amount the net investment return is applied to.
- ¶9 **"Minimum death benefit"** — the guaranteed death benefit (excluding incidental insurance
  benefits) a variable life policy pays whatever the separate account's investment performance.
- ¶10 **"Net investment return"** — the separate account's rate of investment return that is applied
  to the benefit base.
- ¶12 **"Policy processing day"** — the day on which the charges the policy authorises are taken from
  its cash value.
- ¶13 **"Scheduled premium policy"** — a variable life policy whose premium amounts and premium timing
  are both fixed by the insurer; ¶6 **"Flexible premium policy"** — any variable life policy that is not
  a scheduled premium policy within ¶13.
- ¶14 **"Variable death benefit"** — the death benefit (excluding incidental insurance benefits) of a
  variable life policy that depends on the separate account's investment performance, i.e. what the
  insurer would owe if there were no minimum death benefit.

¶¶2, 5, 11 define "Affiliate", "Control" (with a **ten percent (10%)** voting-securities
presumption) and "Person" — corporate-governance vocabulary, not valuation inputs. ¶7 defines
"General account" (all the insurer's assets outside separate accounts), and ¶8 defines "Incidental
insurance benefit" (every benefit other than the variable and minimum death benefits, for example
accidental death and dismemberment, disability, guaranteed insurability options and family income or
term riders); ¶17 uses both to place reserves.

**Valuation Requirements.** ¶15 (p. 1098) is the same pointer as A-250 ¶3 and A-255 ¶4: variable life
reserve liabilities are set as Appendix A-820 provides, by actuarial procedures that recognise the
variable nature of the benefits and any mortality guarantees.

**¶16 is different, and it is the one place in the three separate-account items where a genuine
reserve mechanic is printed.** It is the **guaranteed minimum death benefit** reserve, and unlike
everything else in A-250/A-255/A-270 it is a construction, not a delegation. The opening sentence
(p. 1098), paraphrased: the GMDB reserve is the reserve needed for the contingency that death occurs
while the guaranteed minimum death benefit exceeds the death benefit payable without the guarantee;
it must be **held in the insurer's general account**, and it may not be less than **the greater of two
minimum reserves**, legs a and b.

**Leg a — the one-third depreciation term-cost floor** (¶16.a, p. 1098). Summed over every variable
life contract: the term costs (if any) for one full year from the valuation date — or, if shorter, for
the remaining period of the guarantee not already covered by the separate account reserves — computed
assuming "an immediate one-third depreciation in the current value of the assets in the separate
account", after which the assets earn a net investment return at the assumed investment rate.

**Leg b — the "attained age level" reserve** (¶16.b, pp. 1098–1099), summed over every variable life
contract. The recursion, in the library's notation (the comments paraphrase each printed definition):

```
AttainedAgeLevelReserve(y) = max( 0 , Residue(y) + Payment(y) )                       [¶16.b]
        floored at zero; equals the residue of the prior year's attained age level reserve
        on the contract, raised or lowered by a payment computed on an attained age basis

Residue(y) = max( 0 , [ AttainedAgeLevelReserve(y−1) · (1 + i_v)  −  TabularClaims(y) ]
                       / TabularProbabilityOfSurvival(y) )                            [¶16.b.i]
        floored at zero; prior year's reserve accumulated at the valuation interest rate,
        less tabular claims on the "excess" (if any) of the GMDB over the death benefit
        payable without a guarantee, the net amount divided by the tabular survival probability

Payment(y)  =  ( (A) − (B) − (C) )  /  ä_temporary(attained age, charge-collection period)   [¶16.b.ii]
        (A) = PV of future guaranteed minimum death benefits
        (B) = PV of the future death benefits that would be paid if there were no guarantee
        (C) = the residue (per ¶16.b.i) of the contract's prior-year attained age level reserve
        ä_temporary = valuation-date PV of a temporary life annuity of one per annum
              from the current attained age, over the remaining period in which the contract
              will collect charges for this risk
        if the contract will collect no further charges for this risk:  Payment(y) = (A) − (B) − (C)
```

Two constraints printed with the recursion:

- **Measuring the "excess"** (¶16.b.i, pp. 1098–1099). The excess behind the residue's tabular claims
  is measured on the death benefits that would actually have applied over the preceding year without
  the guarantee, allowing for the reserve assumptions on how death claim payments fall during the year.
- **A cap on the projection rate for (B)** (¶16.b.ii, p. 1099). The future death benefits in (B) are
  projected on a separate account net investment return that may differ from the assumed investment
  rate or the valuation interest rate, but may never exceed **"the maximum interest rate permitted for
  the valuation of life contracts"**.

**Basis and approximation** (¶16.c, p. 1099). The valuation interest rate and mortality table for both
minimum reserves (16 a. and 16 b.) must conform to "acceptable standards for the valuation of life
insurance contracts", and the company may use suitable approximations and estimates — groupings and
averages among them, without limitation. Note the softer wording — "acceptable standards", not the
hard A-820 delegation used in ¶15.

**¶17 — incidental insurance benefits** (p. 1099), paraphrased. The **general account** holds the
reserve liabilities for all fixed incidental insurance benefits and for any guarantees attached to
what the paragraph calls "variable accidental insurance benefits"; a **separate account** holds the
reserve liabilities for every variable aspect of variable incidental insurance benefits; amounts
follow the actuarial procedures appropriate to each benefit. (On "variable **accidental**" see Gaps
and caveats.)

**¶¶18–19 — separate accounts** (p. 1099). ¶18: separate account assets must be valued at least as often
as variable benefits are determined and, in any case, **at least monthly**. ¶19: the same asset-coverage
floor carried by A-250 ¶2 and A-255 ¶7.

## What this settles for the library

### Confirmed

1. **`us/regulatory/statutory-accounting-and-capital.md`, "The reserve hierarchy", row *Universal
   life*: "Model #585 §5 CRVM adaptation (GMP/GMF)" and "NPR routes through VM-A item **A-585**".**
   Substantively confirmed. A-585 is the Commissioners Reserve Valuation Method adapted for UL and it
   is built on the Guaranteed Maturity Premium, the Guaranteed Maturity Fund and the r-ratio, exactly
   as the row asserts. **[R155]** now sources it first-hand.
2. **`products/universal_life/technical-notes.md` (≈ line 381): "Guaranteed Maturity Premium /
   Guaranteed Maturity Fund with the `r = min(1, policy value / GMF)` ratio and the alternative
   minimum reserve test".** All three components confirmed to exist and to be the operative mechanics
   — but the r formula needs the fixed/flexible qualification below.
3. **SSAP No. 51 as the accounting home.** A-585's own header names **SSAP No. 51—Life Contracts**
   and **SSAP No. 56—Separate Accounts** as the relevant SSAPs, consistent with the library's use of
   SSAP No. 51 for life-contract reserves and SSAP No. 56 for separate accounts [REG-R79] [REG-R83].
4. **A-255's relevance to MVA business.** The library's separate-accounts section says a RILA
   separate account may be at book value and may carry its own AVR and IMR [REG-R83]. A-255 ¶5's
   requirement that the separate account liability equal at least the contract-MVA surrender value,
   with an asset transfer to cover any shortfall, is the reserve-side counterpart and is now sourced
   **[R157]**.

### Contradicted or in need of correction

5. **The "not retrieved" caveats are now stale for these four items.** Both
   `us/regulatory/statutory-accounting-and-capital.md` ("**Neither the AG I text nor A-585, A-820 and
   A-830 as printed in the manual was retrieved**", ≈ line 242) and
   `products/universal_life/technical-notes.md` ("the AP&P Appendix A print of A-585 was **not
   retrieved**", ≈ line 393) and `products/indexed_ul/technical-notes.md` ("**A-585, A-820 and
   A-830 were not retrieved**", ≈ line 377) must be narrowed to **A-820 and A-830 only**. A-585,
   A-250, A-255 and A-270 have now been read in full.
6. **`us/regulatory/statutory-accounting-and-capital.md` ≈ line 226 and
   `products/registered_index_linked_annuity/technical-notes.md` ≈ line 679: "the VM-A index's two
   closest formulaic items, A-250 (variable annuities) and A-255 (modified guaranteed annuities), were
   likewise not read".** Now read — **and the finding is that neither is a reserve method.** A-250 is
   three sentences: a separate-account asset floor and a delegation to A-820. A-255 is a delegation to
   A-820 plus an MVA-surrender-value floor, an asset top-up rule and a residual "additional reserve"
   requirement. **Neither contains a formula, a symbol, a factor, a table, or the word CARVM.**
   Calling them "the closest formulaic items" is defensible only in the sense of *nearest by subject
   matter*; they add **nothing** to a RILA formulaic CARVM run beyond the MVA floor. The library
   should say so: **a RILA CARVM run still rests on the SVL text alone, and reading A-250/A-255 does
   not change that.** That was the open question and it is now closed.
7. **The r-ratio formula needs a qualifier.** `r = min(1, policy value / GMF)` is correct **only for
   a flexible premium universal life insurance policy**. A-585 ¶8.d (p. 1103) sets r to one in every
   case except a flexible premium policy whose policy value is below the GMF. **For a fixed premium UL
   policy r ≡ 1 regardless of funding level.**
8. **A-585 does not cite Model #585.** The item's "Relevant NAIC Model Laws/Regulations" line names
   only the **Standard Valuation Law (#820)**. Statements of the form "A-585 *is* Model #585 §5"
   should be softened to "A-585 carries the UL CRVM adaptation, and the AP&P print attributes it to
   the Standard Valuation Law (#820)". The library's own [REG-R5] annotation for Model #585 §5 stays
   valid as a separate source; the two texts were not compared line by line here because Model #585
   was not re-read for this extraction.
9. **`us/regulatory/technical-notes.md`, "Formulaic reserves" — the material gap, and the most
   consequential correction.** That section gives exactly two formulaic constructions: CRVM as the
   SVL §5.A modified-net-premium `k*` engine, and CARVM as the greatest-present-value engine. It then
   says, in the VM-20 section, "**All Other, and IUL where no DR or SR is computed, take the VM-A/VM-C
   formulaic basic reserve** — the CRVM engine above". **For universal life that instruction sends the
   implementer to the wrong engine.** A-585 prescribes a *different* CRVM construction for UL:

   ```
   SVL §5.A engine (in the library today):  V(s) = max(0, APV_s(B) − k*·APV_s(G))
                                            with a uniform percentage k* of CONTRACT gross premiums

   A-585 engine (missing from the library): V(t) = ((A) − (B))·r − (C) − (D)
                                            with (B) driven by the GUARANTEED MATURITY PREMIUM,
                                            not by contract gross premiums
   ```

   The A-585 engine has no `k*`, no 19-pay cap in its own text (it delegates the expense allowance to
   A-820 ¶9 via "(a) − (b)"), and introduces three objects the library's formulaic section does not
   define at all: the **GMP solve**, the **GMF path**, and the **r-ratio**. "Formulaic reserves" needs
   a third subsection.
10. **The Alternative Minimum Reserve test is absent from the library and is not the same thing as a
    deficiency reserve.** `us/regulatory/technical-notes.md`, "Deficiency and other additional
    reserves", defines a deficiency as "where the valuation net premium exceeds the actual gross
    premium collected" and routes it to AG I / Model #830. **A-585 ¶12's comparator is the Guaranteed
    Maturity Premium, not the gross premium**, and its remedy is a *substitution of the GMP for the
    valuation net premium* inside a re-run of the reserve on minimum mortality and interest, then a
    max against the reserve as actually computed. It is a distinct minimum, and it belongs in the
    formulaic section for UL.
11. **Indexed UL gets one directly relevant sentence.** A-585 ¶8.c (p. 1103) computes the GMP at issue
    on the policy's guarantees at issue, "**excluding guarantees linked to an external referent**".
    Read with the ¶4 definition of an interest-indexed UL policy — one whose interest credits are tied
    to an external referent — this means **the index-linked crediting is stripped out of the GMP
    solve entirely** — the GMP is computed on the non-indexed guarantees. `products/indexed_ul/`
    should carry this; it is the only index-specific reserve rule in the item.

### Left open

12. **(a) − (b), and hence the whole CRVM expense allowance for UL, is delegated to Appendix A-820
    ¶9 and cannot be stated from this extraction.** The library's existing SVL §5.A α/β material
    [REG-R1] is the closest thing it has, but A-585 points at **A-820 ¶9**, not at the SVL, and A-820
    was not extracted. Until A-820 is read, the (C) term and the CRVM leg of ¶13's valuation net
    premium remain unimplementable from library sources.
13. **The prescribed mortality table and valuation interest rate for a UL reserve are likewise in
    A-820** (¶¶8.j, 10). A-585 prints no rate, no table and no factor.
14. **Whether A-585 as printed in the March 2026 manual differs from Model #585 §5 as printed in the
    model law** is not settled here — Model #585 was not re-read for this extraction.
15. **The VM-20 §3.B.6 → VM-A → A-585 routing is asserted by VM-20 and VM-A** [REG-R3] [REG-R110],
    **not by A-585.** A-585 contains no reference to VM-20, VM-A, the Valuation Manual, or
    principle-based reserving of any kind.

## Citation anchors

Pinpoints with a one-line paraphrase each (and, where the exact words are the point, a short key phrase
in quotation marks), for downstream citation without re-reading the manual. All from the NAIC *AP&P
Manual, As of March 2026*; the printed wording is at the pages given.

1. **A-585 ¶8, p. 1102** — the UL minimum valuation standard is CRVM as the item adapts it, on the
   tables and interest rates the item specifies.
2. **A-585 ¶8, p. 1102** — the terminal reserve at a policy anniversary, for the basic policy plus any
   benefits or riders with no separate premium, is the net level premium reserve less (C) less (D).
3. **A-585 ¶8.a / ¶8.a.i, p. 1102** — net level premium reserve = ((A) − (B))·r, with (A) the
   present value of all future guaranteed benefits "at the date of valuation".
4. **A-585 ¶8.b, p. 1103** — ä_x and ä_{x+t} are annuities of one per year on policy anniversaries,
   running to "the highest attained age at which a premium may be paid".
5. **A-585 ¶8.c, p. 1103** — flexible premium GMP: the level gross premium, payable over the permitted
   premium period, that matures the policy for an amount consistent with the policy structure on the
   latest permitted maturity date (else the highest age of the valuation mortality table).
6. **A-585 ¶8.c, p. 1103** — the GMP is computed at issue on all guarantees at issue, "excluding
   guarantees linked to an external referent".
7. **A-585 ¶8.c fn. 2, p. 1103** — the GMP is corridor-adjusted and may fall short of the premium needed
   to pay all charges, especially in year one for policies with heavy first-year expense charges.
8. **A-585 ¶8.d, p. 1103** — r = 1, except a flexible premium policy with policy value below the GMF,
   where r = policy value / GMF.
9. **A-585 ¶8.e, p. 1103** — the GMF at any duration is the amount that, with future GMPs, matures the
   policy on the guarantees at issue.
10. **A-585 ¶8.i, p. 1104** — future guaranteed benefits project the greater of the GMF and the policy
    value, with future GMPs and every policy or declared guarantee, plus guaranteed benefits that do not
    depend on the policy value.
11. **A-585 ¶8.j, p. 1104** — interest and mortality are those A-820 specifies "for policies issued in
    the same year", plus any tables needed for rider benefits valued with the policy.
12. **A-585 ¶11, p. 1104** — of several consistent methods for structural changes, the simplest may be
    to keep GMF and GMP proportional to the current face amount (per-dollar values times the new face).
13. **A-585 ¶12, p. 1104** — AMR trigger: in any policy year, GMP below the valuation net premium
    (method actually used, minimum mortality and interest); the minimum reserve is then the greater of
    legs a. and b.
14. **A-585 ¶12.b, p. 1105** — leg b substitutes the GMP for the valuation net premium in each policy
    year in which the valuation net premium exceeds the GMP.
15. **A-250 ¶3, p. 1095** — the variable annuity reserve is set under Appendix A-820 by procedures
    recognising "the variable nature of the benefits provided and any mortality guarantees". *(This is
    the whole of A-250's reserve content.)*
16. **A-255 ¶5, p. 1096** — the separate account liability is at least the surrender value under the
    contract's own MVA formula, and any further reserve needed for future guaranteed benefits must be
    set up.
17. **A-255 ¶1, p. 1096** — the underlying assets must be held in a separate account for as long as the
    contract can be surrendered.
18. **A-270 ¶16.a, p. 1098** — the term-cost floor assumes "an immediate one-third depreciation" of the
    separate account assets, then a net investment return equal to the assumed investment rate.
19. **A-270 ¶16.b.ii, p. 1099** — the separate account return used to project (B) is capped at "the
    maximum interest rate permitted for the valuation of life contracts".
20. **A-270 ¶18, p. 1099** — separate account assets must be valued at least as often as variable
    benefits are determined and, in any event, "at least monthly".

## Gaps and caveats

### What the documents do not address

- **No effective dates anywhere.** Restated because it is the negative finding most likely to be
  mis-remembered later: none of the four items carries an effective date, operative date,
  applicability threshold, transition rule or grandfathering clause. Do not attribute a date to
  A-585, A-250, A-255 or A-270.
- **A-585 carries the *valuation* half of the UL model regulation only.** The extracted pages contain
  definitions and valuation requirements and nothing else — **no nonforfeiture provisions, no
  mandatory policy provisions, no annual-report-to-policyowner requirements, and no separate
  interest-indexed UL section**. Model #585's own interest-indexed UL requirements (filing,
  demonstrations, the external referent) are **not** in this appendix print; the only indexed-UL rule
  here is the ¶8.c exclusion of externally-referenced guarantees from the GMP. Anything the library
  says about UL nonforfeiture must continue to cite Model #585 [REG-R5], not A-585.
- **No numbers.** A-585 prints no mortality table, no interest rate, no factor and no threshold. A-250,
  A-255 and A-270 print none either, apart from A-270 ¶5's 10% control presumption (governance, not
  valuation) and A-270 ¶16.a's "one-third depreciation".
- **No worked examples in any of the four items.** ¶11's proportionality method is the only
  quasi-procedural passage, and it is offered permissively ("Several such methods are possible").
- **Nothing on VM-20, VM-A, VM-C or PBR.** The routing that makes A-585 matter to a post-operative-date
  net premium reserve is asserted elsewhere [REG-R3 §3.B.6] [REG-R110]; A-585 is silent on it.
- **Nothing on reinsurance, deficiency reserves, secondary guarantees, shadow accounts, AG 38 or
  Model #830.** A-585 is a standalone UL CRVM adaptation; ULSG machinery is not in it.
- **A-250 and A-255 contain no CARVM adaptation, no elective-benefit path enumeration, no interim
  value rule and no index-crediting rule.** For an index-linked separate account contract they are
  scope definitions plus, in A-255, an MVA floor.

### Extraction artefacts and ambiguities

The PDF text layer for these pages has **irregular line breaks mid-word and mid-phrase**, and it
**loses fraction bars, superscripts and diacritics**. Specifics, so a later reader can judge what is
solid and what is reconstructed. Items 2 and 3 describe the 2026-08-06 extraction; the text layer
re-extracted for the 2026-10-25 check shows neither, while items 1, 4 and 5 recur in it:

1. **Fraction bars are lost.** ¶8.a.ii extracts as "(B) is the quantity PVFB äx+t where PVFB is …"
   with a bare "äx" on the following line; ¶8.f as "(C) is the quantity ((a)-(b))äx+t r where …" with
   "äx" on the following line; ¶13 as "PVFB / äx" and "PVFB + (a)-(b) / äx  äx" laid out over two
   lines. The **reading** — (B) = (PVFB/ä_x)·ä_{x+t}, (C) = ((a)−(b))·(ä_{x+t}/ä_x)·r, CRVM VNP =
   PVFB/ä_x + ((a)−(b))/ä_x — is the only arrangement consistent with the surviving token order and
   with (A)−(B) being a prospective net level reserve, but the **denominators are inferred from
   layout, not read from a bar character**. Anyone relying on the exact placement of `r` in (C) should
   re-open p. 1103.

   "supplem entary", "meth od", "th e", "declar ed", "prem iums", "guar anteed", "ca lculated",

   artefacts only; no text is missing. The short quotations kept above have had them silently
