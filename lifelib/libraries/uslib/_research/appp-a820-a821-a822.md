## Scope and applicability

### What business A-820 governs

Per **A-820 ¶5** (life) and **¶6** (annuities), the appendix sets the **minimum standard of valuation** for:

- all **life insurance and endowment** policies and contracts (¶5);
- **individual annuity and pure endowment contracts**, and **annuities and pure endowments bought under group
  annuity and pure endowment contracts** (¶6);
- **disability and accidental death benefits** in or supplementary to policies (¶5.d, ¶5.e, ¶13.c);
- **group life, substandard life and other special benefits**, for which ¶5.f prescribes only
  "tables which provide for an adequate reserve";
- **accident and health insurance contracts**, by cross-reference only (¶22);
- and, from ¶23 onward, everything issued on or after the Valuation Manual operative date, by delegation to the
  Valuation Manual.

**A definitional point that changes scope.** ¶1.e (PDF p. 1186 [A820–1]) defines "life insurance" as contracts
carrying mortality risk, and expressly counts annuity and pure endowment contracts among them (together with whatever
the Valuation Manual specifies). So in A-820's PBR half, **annuities are inside "life insurance"**. But ¶1's own
preamble limits when these definitions bite — see the next subsection — and the pre-operative-date half (¶¶5–22)
plainly treats life (¶5, ¶¶11–13) and annuities (¶6, ¶¶14–15) as separate tracks. Do not carry ¶1.e's definition
backwards into ¶¶5–22.

### Effective dates

- **The definitions clause (¶1, opening words; PDF p. 1186 [A820–1]).** The ¶1 definitions apply only "on or after
  the operative date of the Valuation Manual".
- **The forward applicability rule (¶3; PDF p. 1187 [A820–2]).** ¶¶23–27 govern every policy and contract issued on
  or after "the January 1, 2017, operative date of the Valuation Manual".
- **The grandfathering rule (¶4; PDF pp. 1187–1188 [A820–2/3]).** ¶¶5–22 govern, as appropriate, the policies and
  contracts that were subject to the appendix before that same January 1, 2017 operative date, and ¶¶23–27 are
  expressly disapplied for all of them.
- **The Valuation Manual as the minimum standard (¶23; PDF p. 1196 [A820–11]).** For a policy issued on or after the
  operative date, the minimum standard of valuation that ¶3 calls for is whatever standard the Valuation Manual
  prescribes.
- **The interest-rate applicability threshold (¶7, opening words; PDF p. 1189 [A820–4]).** The calendar year statutory
  valuation interest rates defined in ¶7 are the rates for the minimum standard on policies issued on or after "the
  effective date of the Codification".
- **The A&H split (¶22; PDF p. 1196 [A820–11]).** Two sentences. A&H contracts issued on or after the operative date
  take the Valuation Manual's standard as their ¶3 minimum. Contracts of the bracketed kinds — the brackets read
  "[disability, accident and sickness, accident and health]" — issued in a window starting January 1, 2017 and ending
  before the operative date take whatever standard the commissioner adopts by regulation.

The square brackets in ¶22 are **printed in the manual** — a model-law drafting alternative left in place. Note that
¶22 as printed is internally odd: its second sentence posits a window "on or after January 1, 2017, and prior to the
operative date" while ¶3 and ¶4 fix that operative date **at** January 1, 2017, leaving the window empty. Record this as
printed; do not reconcile it.

### Mortality-table effective dates (A-820 ¶5)

All on PDF pp. 1188–1189 [A820–3/4].

- **¶5.a** — ordinary life issued on the standard basis **on or after January 1, 2004**, leaving out preneed policies
  (sent to Appendix A-817) and leaving out disability and accidental death benefits (the printed clause runs the two
  exclusions together with no conjunction) → the **Commissioners 2001 Standard Ordinary Mortality Table**; at company
  election for one or more **specified plans**, the **2001 CSO with 25-Year Select Mortality Factors**; or any later
  ordinary table the NAIC adopts for valuing such policies (¶5.a.iii, "adopted subsequently by the NAIC").
- **¶5.b** — ordinary life issued on the standard basis **before January 1, 2004**, disability and accidental death
  benefits again left out, but with **preneed policies issued on or after January 1, 2012** brought in (cross-referring
  to A-817) → the **Commissioners 1980 Standard Ordinary Mortality Table**; at company election for specified plans,
  the **1980 CSO with Ten-Year Select Mortality Factors**.
- **¶5.c** — industrial life, standard basis, excluding disability and AD benefits → **Commissioners 1961 Standard
  Industrial Mortality Table** or any industrial table **adopted after 1980** by the NAIC.
- **¶5.d** — total and permanent disability benefits in or supplementary to ordinary policies → the SOA's **1952
  Disability Study** tables, namely its Period 2 disablement rates together with its 1930–1950 termination rates,
  applied "with due regard to the type of benefit", or any disablement and termination tables the NAIC adopted after
  1980; **for active lives, combined with a mortality table permitted for life insurance reserves**.
- **¶5.e** — accidental death benefits → the **1959 Accidental Death Benefits Table** or any adopted after 1980;
  **combined with a mortality table** for life insurance reserves.
- **¶5.f** — group life, substandard, other special benefits → "tables which provide for an adequate reserve".

**Note what ¶5 does *not* name.** The **2017 CSO** is nowhere in A-820's printed text. It can only enter through
¶5.a.iii's forward reference to tables "adopted subsequently by the NAIC" — and in practice reaches post-2017 issues
through the Valuation Manual under ¶23 instead.

### Annuity mortality effective dates (A-821)

All on PDF p. 1200 [A821–2].

- **¶10** — subject to the ¶12 exception, the **Annuity 2000 Mortality Table** sets the minimum standard of valuation
  for individual annuity and pure endowment contracts **issued from January 1, 2001 through December 31, 2014**.
- **¶11** — subject to the same exception, the **2012 IAR Mortality Table** sets it for individual annuity and pure
  endowment contracts **issued on or after January 1, 2015**.
- **¶12** — for an **individual** annuity or pure endowment contract, the **1983 Table "a" without projection** is the
  minimum standard "solely when the contract is based on life contingencies" and funds periodic benefits arising from
  (a) court or out-of-court **settlements of tort claims**; (b) settlements of **similar actions, workers' compensation
  claims for example**; or (c) settlements of **long-term disability claims** in which a temporary or life annuity
  takes the place of continuing disability payments — i.e. the **structured settlement** carve-out.
- **¶15** — the **1994 GAR Table** sets the minimum standard for any annuity or pure endowment bought under a **group**
  annuity or pure endowment contract. **No effective date is printed for ¶15.**

### Transition and grandfathering language — the whole of what is printed

A-820 contains **exactly two** transition provisions, both summarised above: **¶3** (PBR provisions apply to issues
on or after 1/1/2017) and **¶4** (pre-1/1/2017 issues stay on ¶¶5–22, and ¶¶23–27 are expressly disapplied for every
one of them). There is **no elective-transition window, no phase-in, no "mandatory from" date and no company
election** anywhere in A-820's text. The only place A-820 anticipates transition machinery is by delegation —
**¶24.e** requires the Valuation Manual to specify, among other things, *"transition rules and internal controls"*.

## Mechanics

### 1. Definitions that carry weight (A-820 ¶1)

Eleven terms (¶1.a–k, PDF pp. 1186–1187 [A820–1/2]), applying **on or after the Valuation Manual operative date**.
The ones an implementer must hold:

| Term | Definition (paraphrased; short key phrases quoted) |
|---|---|
| **accident and health insurance** (¶1.a) | contracts carrying **morbidity risk** that protect against economic loss from accident, sickness or medical conditions, plus whatever the Valuation Manual specifies |
| **appointed actuary** (¶1.b) | the qualified actuary the company appoints, under the Valuation Manual's rules, to prepare the actuarial opinion |
| **company** (¶1.c) | an entity that has written, issued or reinsured life, A&H or deposit-type contracts in any U.S. state, district or territory and **still has at least one of them in force or on claim**; or one that has done so and must hold a certificate of authority for such business |
| **deposit-type contract** (¶1.d) | contracts carrying **neither mortality nor morbidity risk**, plus whatever the Valuation Manual specifies |
| **life insurance** (¶1.e) | contracts carrying **mortality risk, annuity and pure endowment contracts expressly included** |
| **policyholder behavior** (¶1.g) | any action open under a contract to the policyholder, contract holder or anyone else entitled to elect options (a certificate holder, for instance); the non-exhaustive printed list runs lapse, withdrawal, transfer, deposit, premium payment, loan, annuitization and benefit elections; **mortality and morbidity events are carved out** ("excluding events of mortality or morbidity") where the benefits they trigger are fixed in essence by the contract terms |
| **principle-based valuation** (¶1.h) | a reserve valuation using at least one method or assumption **set by the insurer**, and bound by ¶¶25–27 as the Valuation Manual specifies |
| **qualified actuary** (¶1.i) | qualified to sign the applicable actuarial opinion under **American Academy of Actuaries** qualification standards and meeting the Valuation Manual's requirements |
| **tail risk** (¶1.j) | risk arising either because low-probability events happen more often than a normal distribution predicts, or because events of very large size or magnitude are observed |
| **Valuation Manual** (¶1.k) | the NAIC-adopted manual of valuation instructions, as this appendix specifies and as later amended |

**The ¶1.g exclusion is the operative one for a model:** mortality and morbidity are **not** policyholder behaviour, so
the VM-31/VM-G assumption-governance obligations that attach to policyholder behaviour do not attach to the mortality
basis by this route. **¶1.d/¶1.e together are the SSAP No. 51 vs. No. 52 boundary in valuation clothing** — presence of
mortality or morbidity risk, decided on the contract's terms.

### 2. The four general valuation requirements (A-820 ¶2)

Reserves reported in the financial statements must (¶2.a–d, PDF p. 1187 [A820–2]):

- **a.** be computed under "presently accepted actuarial standards";
- **b.** rest on assumptions giving reserves **no smaller than any contract provision on reserve basis and method
  demands**, consistent with every other contract provision; and the reported reserves and related actuarial items,
  judged against the company's assets backing them (the investment earnings on those assets and the considerations
  expected to be received and retained under the contracts both counted), **must adequately provide for the company's
  obligations under the contracts — benefits and the associated expenses among them**;
- **c.** be computed on assumptions **consistent with those behind the corresponding items in the preceding year-end
  annual statement**, any departure being disclosed in the financial-statement notes;
- **d.** make provision for every actuarial reserve and related statement item that should be set up.

¶2.b is the textual root of asset adequacy analysis inside A-820 itself; A-822 then makes the additional reserve
mandatory. ¶2.c is the disclosure hook behind change-in-valuation-basis reporting.

### 3. CRVM — the commissioners reserve valuation method (A-820 ¶¶11–13)

**¶11, the core construction (PDF pp. 1193–1194 [A820–8/9]), applies to policies with a "uniform amount of
insurance" and "uniform premiums".** Unless the appendix provides otherwise, the CRVM reserve for the life insurance
and endowment benefits of such a policy is the valuation-date present value of the future guaranteed benefits less the
then present value of the future modified net premiums, counted only when positive — "the excess, if any".

The modified net premiums are a single **uniform percentage of the respective contract premiums**, set so that at issue
their present value equals the present value of the policy's benefits **plus "the excess of a. over b."**, where:

- **a.** is a net level annual premium: the issue-date present value of the benefits provided **after the first policy
  year**, divided by the issue-date present value of a one-per-annum annuity paid on the first and each later policy
  anniversary on which a premium is due. It is **capped at the net level annual premium of a nineteen-year-premium
  whole life plan** for the same amount of insurance, taken **at an issue age one year older** than the policy's own.
- **b.** is the one-year term net premium covering the first policy year's benefits.

**Symbols, as an implementer needs them.** Contract issued at age *x*, guaranteed benefit stream *B*, **contract**
gross premiums *G<sub>k</sub>* at durations *k* = 0, 1, 2, …, valuation basis (mortality per ¶5, interest per ¶¶7–10):

```
ä_0  = APV at issue of an annuity of 1 per annum payable on the first and each subsequent
       anniversary on which a premium falls due                                       [¶11.a denominator]
α    = min(  APV_0( benefits provided for AFTER the first policy year ) / ä_0 ,
             P19(x+1)  )                                                              [¶11.a, with the cap]
P19(x+1) = net level annual premium on the NINETEEN-year premium whole life plan, same amount,
       at age x+1  (note: age x+1, not x)                                             [¶11.a proviso]
β    = net one-year term premium for the first policy year's benefits                     [¶11.b]
E    = α − β                                                          (the "excess of a. over b.")
k*   : APV_0( k*·G ) = APV_0(B) + E      ⇒    k* = ( APV_0(B) + E ) / APV_0(G)
V_CRVM(s) = max( 0 , APV_s(B) − k*·APV_s(G) )                    ("the excess, if any")
```

Two structural checks fall straight out of the printed text: the cap is at **age x+1**, and `APV_0(B) − k*·APV_0(G) = −E`,
so the pre-floor reserve at issue equals the negative of the expense allowance.

**¶12 — the excess-first-year-premium rule (PDF p. 1194 [A820–9]).** Trigger: a life policy whose first-year
**contract premium** is larger than the second year's, with no comparable extra first-year benefit to account for the
difference, and which provides an endowment benefit, a cash surrender value or both **in an amount larger than that
excess premium**. The paragraph itself defines the **assumed ending date** as the first policy anniversary at which
the endowment benefit plus any cash surrender value then available exceeds the excess premium. At each anniversary
**up to and including** the assumed ending date — except as ¶¶19–20 otherwise provide — the reserve is the
**greater** of the ¶11 reserve and a second ¶11-style reserve computed with four changes:

1. the value defined in "that paragraph" is **cut by 15% of the excess first-year premium**;
2. all present values of benefits and premiums **ignore whatever the policy provides after the assumed ending date**;
3. the policy is **treated as maturing as an endowment on that date**;
4. the **cash surrender value available on that date counts as the endowment benefit**.

The comparison is made on the ¶5 mortality and the ¶¶7–10 interest bases.

**Ambiguity to record, not to resolve.** ¶12 compares the reserve computed as in "the preceding paragraph" with one
computed as in "those paragraphs", whose change (i) cuts "the value defined in that paragraph" by 15%. The
singular/plural mismatch is a renumbering artefact: the model law reads "Subsection A" and "Subsection A(1)". Under
the model law the 15% reduction attaches specifically to **item
a.** — the net level annual premium *α*. **A-820 as printed does not disambiguate it to ¶11.a.** Anyone implementing
from A-820 alone should note the reading is supplied by the model law, not by this text.

**¶13 — extension by consistency (PDF p. 1194 [A820–9]).** CRVM reserves for the following are computed by a method
**consistent with the principles of ¶¶11 and 12**:

- **a.** life policies with a **varying amount of insurance** or **varying premiums**;
- **b.** **group annuity and pure endowment contracts bought under a retirement or deferred-compensation plan** that an
  employer (partnerships and sole proprietorships included), an employee organization, or both, set up or maintain —
  **except** plans providing IRAs or individual retirement annuities under **IRC §408**;
- **c.** disability and accidental death benefits in **every** policy and contract;
- **d.** every remaining benefit — that is, all benefits apart from the life insurance and endowment benefits of life
  policies and the benefits of all other annuity and pure endowment contracts.

**¶13.b is a scope fact worth surfacing:** qualified-plan group annuity and pure endowment business is valued on a
**CRVM-consistent** method, **not** CARVM — ¶14 excludes exactly that population from ¶15. ¶13.a is the hook that makes
CRVM reach **universal life and varying-benefit designs** at all.

### 4. CARVM — the commissioners annuity reserve method (A-820 ¶¶14–15)

**¶14, the scope gate (PDF p. 1194 [A820–9]).** ¶15 governs every annuity and pure endowment contract **except**
group annuity and pure endowment contracts bought under an employer's or employee organization's (or both)
retirement or deferred-compensation plan — the same population as ¶13.b, with the same carve-back for plans
providing IRAs or individual retirement annuities under IRC §408 ("as now or hereafter amended").

**¶15, the construction (PDF p. 1195 [A820–10]).** For annuity and pure endowment benefits, disability and
accidental death benefits excluded, the CARVM reserve is the **largest, over all contract-year ends, of this
difference**: the valuation-date present value of the future guaranteed benefits the contract provides at the end of
that contract year — **guaranteed nonforfeiture benefits included** — minus the valuation-date present value of the
future **valuation considerations** (derived from the future gross considerations the contract requires) falling due
**before** that year end. Two further sentences fix the inputs: the future guaranteed benefits are computed on the
mortality table, if any, and the interest rate or rates **that the contract itself specifies for its guarantees**; and
the valuation considerations are the parts of each gross consideration that the contract applies in determining
nonforfeiture values.

**Symbols.** For each contract-year end *k* = 1 … *H*:

```
X(k) = APV_τ( guaranteed benefits, INCLUDING guaranteed nonforfeiture benefits,
              provided at the end of contract year k )
     − APV_τ( valuation considerations — the nonforfeiture portions of the gross considerations
              the contract requires — falling due BEFORE the end of contract year k )
V_CARVM(τ) = max over k of X(k)
```

Two prescriptions inside ¶15 that a model must honour and that are easy to lose:

- the **future guaranteed benefits** are projected on **the contract's own guarantee basis — the mortality table (if
  any) and interest rate(s) it uses to fix its guaranteed benefits** — not on the valuation basis. The valuation basis
  (¶¶7–10 interest, A-821 mortality per ¶6) enters through the **discounting**;
- **"valuation considerations"** are not gross considerations — they are only the share of each gross consideration
  that the contract terms apply in setting nonforfeiture values.

The implementation obligation is **enumeration of every guaranteed-benefit path** to the end of every contract year;
omitting a path can only understate the reserve. A-820 gives no path list — that is what AG 33 and AG 35 supply, and
neither is in the extracted material.

### 5. Minimum standard for annuities — the mortality leg (A-820 ¶6)

For individual annuity and pure endowment contracts, and for annuities and pure endowments bought under group
contracts, the minimum standard of valuation combines three things: the CARVM methods of ¶¶14–15, the valuation
interest rates of ¶¶7–10, and "the tables defined in Appendix A-821" (¶6, PDF p. 1189 [A820–4]).

So the CARVM triple is: **method = ¶¶14–15, interest = ¶¶7–10, mortality = A-821**. A-821 is not optional colour; it is
the mortality leg by direct cross-reference.

### 6. Valuation interest rate (A-820 ¶¶7–10)

**¶7.a.i — the dynamic formula (PDF p. 1189 [A820–4]).** The calendar year statutory valuation interest rate *I* is
computed by one of two formulas and then rounded "to the nearer one-quarter of one percent". In the library's
symbols (the fraction bar under the second *W* is lost in the text layer — the "2" sits on the following line):

```
(a) Life insurance:
        I = .03 + W·(R1 − .03) + (W/2)·(R2 − .09)

(b) SPIAs, and life-contingent annuity benefits arising from other annuities or GICs
    that have cash settlement options:
        I = .03 + W·(R − .03)

    R1 = min(R, .09)    R2 = max(R, .09)
    R  = reference interest rate (¶9)    W = weighting factor (¶8)
```

**Routing rules ¶7.a.i(c)–(e), which formula applies to what:**

| Contract class | Basis | Formula used |
|---|---|---|
| Life insurance | — | (a) |
| SPIAs; life-contingent annuity benefits arising from other annuities or GICs that have cash settlement options | — | (b), with **W = .80** |
| Other annuities with cash settlement options and GICs with cash settlement options, **guarantee duration in excess of ten (10) years** | **issue year** | (a) — the life insurance formula |
| Other annuities with cash settlement options and GICs with cash settlement options, **guarantee duration of ten (10) years or less** | **issue year** | (b) |
| Other annuities with **no** cash settlement options and GICs with **no** cash settlement options | (issue year — forced, per ¶8.c.vi) | (b) |
| Other annuities with cash settlement options and GICs with cash settlement options | **change in fund** | (b) |

**¶7.a.ii — the stability rule (PDF p. 1190 [A820–5]).** Compute the life insurance rate for an issue year as if this
rule did not exist. If the result differs by "less than one-half of one percent (1/2 of 1%)" from the actual rate that
applied to similar policies issued the calendar year before, the life rate for the year is set equal to that prior-year
actual rate.

Note this is a **life-only** rule and it references the *actual* prior-year rate, so the recursion is on the published
series, not on a freshly recomputed one.

**¶8.a — weighting factors for life insurance (PDF p. 1190 [A820–5]).** Three guarantee-duration bands: **.50** for a
guarantee duration of 10 years or less, **.45** for more than 10 but not more than 20 years, and **.35** for more than
20 years. These three values are kept here because the R153 annotation in
`references/regulatory-and-actuarial-references.md` states them. For life insurance the **guarantee duration** is
the longest period the insurance can stay in force on a basis the policy guarantees — counting any option to convert
to plans whose premium rates, nonforfeiture values or both the original policy guarantees.

**¶8.b — weighting factor for SPIAs** and for life-contingent annuity benefits arising from other annuities or GICs
that have cash settlement options (PDF p. 1190 [A820–5]): **.80** — the value the immediate-annuity documents rely
on (`products/immediate_annuity/product-spec.md` and `products/immediate_annuity/technical-notes.md`).

**¶8.c.i — weighting factors for other annuities and GICs on an issue year basis, by Plan Type (PDF p. 1191
[A820–6]).** A grid of four guarantee-duration bands (5 years or less; more than 5 but not more than 10; more than 10
but not more than 20; more than 20) by three Plan Types (A, B, C) — twelve factors, ranging from .35 to .80. At every
duration Plan Type A carries the highest factor, and within each Plan Type the factor falls or stays level as the
guarantee duration lengthens. The grid is **cited, not reproduced**: read it at the pinpoint, or in the Standard
Valuation Law model print, which the library cites for the same weighting-factor machinery at [REG-R1 §4b.C(1)(c)]

**¶8.c.ii — change in fund basis:** the ¶8.c.i factors **increased by** **A +.15, B +.25, C +.05** (PDF p. 1191
[A820–6]; increments kept because the R153 annotation in `references/regulatory-and-actuarial-references.md` states
them).

**¶8.c.iii — the no-forward-guarantee increment (PDF p. 1191 [A820–6]):** a further **+.05 for each of Plan Types A, B
and C** is added to the item i. factor (or the item ii. derived factor) in two cases — **issue-year-basis** contracts
that have cash settlement options but give no interest guarantee on considerations received **more than one year after
issue or purchase**; and **change-in-fund-basis** contracts that give no interest-rate guarantee on considerations
received **more than twelve (12) months beyond the valuation date**. (Kept because the R153 annotation in
`references/regulatory-and-actuarial-references.md` states the further +.05.)

**¶8.c.iv — guarantee duration for annuities/GICs (PDF pp. 1191–1192 [A820–6/7]):** for those **with** cash
settlement options, the number of years over which the contract guarantees interest **above the calendar year
statutory valuation rate for life policies whose guarantee duration exceeds twenty (20) years**; for those with
**no** cash settlement options, the number of years from issue or purchase to the scheduled start of annuity
benefits.

**¶8.c.v — Plan Type definitions (PDF p. 1192 [A820–7]):**

- **Plan Type A:** at any time, the policyholder can take funds out only (1) with an adjustment reflecting changes in
  interest rates or asset values since the insurer received the funds, (2) without adjustment but in instalments over
  five years or more, (3) as an immediate life annuity, or (4) not at all. (The first item is printed "(l)", a
  lower-case L.)
- **Plan Type B:** before the interest rate guarantee expires, withdrawal only (1) with such an adjustment, (2) without
  adjustment in instalments over five years or more, or (3) not at all; **once the guarantee ends, funds can be taken
  without adjustment as a lump sum or in instalments over less than five years**.
- **Plan Type C:** before the guarantee expires, funds can be taken as a lump sum or in instalments over less than five
  years, either (1) with no interest-rate or asset-value adjustment, or (2) **subject only to a fixed surrender charge
  that the contract sets as a percentage of the fund**.

**¶8.c.vi — the issue year / change in fund election (PDF p. 1192 [A820–7]):** a company **may elect** either basis
for GICs and annuities that have cash settlement options; GICs and other annuities **without cash settlement options
are confined to the issue year basis**. Under an **issue year basis**, the minimum-standard rate for the contract's
**entire duration** is the calendar year valuation rate of the year of issue or purchase; under a **change in fund
basis**, **each change in the fund** takes the calendar year valuation rate of the year in which that change occurs.

|---|---|---|
| **a.** Life insurance | **the lesser of** the 36-month average and the 12-month average | the calendar year **preceding** the year of issue |
| **b.** SPIAs; life-contingent annuity benefits arising from other annuities/GICs with cash settlement options | 12-month average | the calendar year **of** issue or purchase |
| **c.** Other annuities/GICs with cash settlement options, **year of issue** basis, **guarantee duration in excess of ten (10) years** | **the lesser of** the 36-month and 12-month averages | the calendar year **of** issue or purchase |

| **e.** Other annuities/GICs with **no** cash settlement options | 12-month average | the calendar year **of** issue or purchase |
| **f.** Other annuities/GICs with cash settlement options, **change in fund** basis | 12-month average | the calendar year **of the change in the fund** |

**Implementation consequence.** ¶10 means the reference series must be a **configurable table keyed by calendar year**,
never a hard-coded Moody's feed. ¶8.c.vi means the interest basis is a **per-contract election recorded at issue**, and
the change-in-fund basis makes the valuation rate a function of **the year each increment of fund arose** — a per-layer
attribute, not a per-contract scalar.

### 7. Minimum reserves, optional higher standards (A-820 ¶¶16–18)

**¶16 — the aggregate nonforfeiture-basis floor (PDF p. 1195 [A820–10]).** A company's aggregate reserves for all its
life insurance policies, disability and accidental death benefits excluded, must in no event fall below the aggregate
that the methods of ¶¶11–15 and ¶¶19–21 produce when run on **the mortality table(s) and interest rate(s) the policies
use to calculate their nonforfeiture benefits**.

This is an **aggregate** test across all life insurance policies, not seriatim, and it re-runs the *same methods* on the
*nonforfeiture* mortality and interest basis.

**¶17 — optional higher standard, with its constraint (PDF p. 1195 [A820–10]).** A company may choose to reserve any
category of policies, contracts or benefits on any standard that yields greater aggregate reserves for that category
than the minimum standard — **but for policies and contracts other than annuity and pure endowment contracts, the
interest rate(s) it uses may not exceed the corresponding rate(s) used to calculate any nonforfeiture benefits those
policies or contracts provide**.

**¶18 — reverting to a lower standard, with the appointed-actuary carve-out (PDF p. 1195 [A820–10]).** A company that
has at any time adopted a standard producing greater aggregate reserves than the minimum may later move to a lower
standard, though never below the minimum; and **holding additional reserves that the appointed actuary previously
determined is not to be treated as adopting a higher standard of valuation**.

The ¶18 proviso, paired with **A-822 ¶4**, is the rule that keeps asset adequacy additional reserves **outside** the
change-in-valuation-basis machinery in both directions.

### 8. Deficiency reserves — the formulaic construction (A-820 ¶¶19–20)

This is the piece the library previously had only by reference. **¶19 (PDF p. 1195 [A820–10]).** The trigger: in some
contract year, the gross premium the company charges is below the valuation net premium computed by the reserve's own
method but on the minimum valuation standards of mortality and interest. The consequence: the minimum reserve is
**the greater of (i) the reserve on the mortality table, interest rate and method actually used, and (ii) a reserve by
the method actually used but on the minimum mortality and interest standards, with the actual gross premium put in
place of the valuation net premium in every contract year where the valuation net premium is the larger**. The
minimum standards meant are those of ¶5 (mortality) and ¶¶7–10 (interest).

As a procedure:

```
TRIGGER   in any contract year:  G(k) < π_min(k)
          where π_min(k) = valuation net premium computed by the METHOD ACTUALLY USED for the
          policy, but on the MINIMUM valuation standards of mortality (¶5) and interest (¶¶7-10)

V_actual  = reserve on the mortality table, rate of interest, and method ACTUALLY USED
V_def     = reserve by the METHOD ACTUALLY USED, on the MINIMUM standards of mortality and
            interest, with the valuation net premium REPLACED BY the actual gross premium G(k)
            in each contract year k for which π_min(k) > G(k)

MinimumReserve = max( V_actual , V_def )
```

Note precisely what is substituted: the **actual gross premium**, only **in the deficient years**, inside a reserve that
is otherwise on the **minimum** mortality and interest standards with the **method actually used**. There is no separate
"deficiency reserve" quantity in ¶19 — the deficiency is expressed as a **floor on the policy reserve**.

**¶20 — interaction with the excess-first-year-premium designs (PDF pp. 1195–1196 [A820–10/11]).** For the same
design ¶12 describes — first-year premium above the second year's, no comparable extra first-year benefit, and an
endowment benefit and/or cash surrender value larger than the excess (¶20 words the trigger on the **gross** premium
where ¶12 uses the **contract** premium) — ¶¶19–20 are applied as though the ¶11 method were the method actually used
for the reserve, and at each policy anniversary the minimum reserve is **the greater of the ¶¶11–12 minimum reserve
and the ¶¶19–20 minimum reserve**.

So for this design: substitute **CRVM (¶11)** as "the method actually used" inside the ¶19 test, then take
`max( min-reserve per ¶¶11–12 , min-reserve per ¶¶19–20 )`. **¶20 refers to itself** ("the provisions of paragraphs 19
and 20") — a renumbering artefact from the model law's "Subsection A and this Subsection B"; read it as "¶19 and this
¶20".

### 9. Indeterminate premium plans (A-820 ¶21)

For a plan of life insurance providing for **future premium determination** by the company based on then-estimates of
future experience, **or** any life or annuity plan whose minimum reserves the methods above cannot determine, the
reserves held under the plan must (¶21, PDF p. 1196 [A820–11]):

- **a.** be appropriate to the plan's benefits and its pattern of premiums; and
- **b.** be computed by a method consistent with this appendix's principles.

This is the catch-all that keeps a formulaic reserve computable for designs ¶¶11–15 do not describe.

### 10. What the Valuation Manual must specify (A-820 ¶24) — the PBR/formulaic boundary

This paragraph is the textual authority behind the library's statement that whole life and ordinary UL run the old CRVM
calculation inside a PBR-era manual.

**¶24.a — minimum valuation standards the Valuation Manual must specify (PDF p. 1196 [A820–11]).** The Valuation
Manual must define ¶3 business and set its minimum valuation standards, and those standards are to be:
**i. CRVM** for life insurance contracts (printed as "life insurance contracts, or other than annuity contracts");
**ii. CARVM** for annuity contracts; and **iii.** minimum reserves for every other ¶3 policy or contract.

**¶24.b** — which policies are subject to a principle-based valuation under ¶25, and minimum valuation standards
consistent with those requirements.

**¶24.c** (PDF pp. 1196–1197 [A820–11/12]) — for PBR business: report format requirements under ¶26.c; prescribed
assumptions for the risks the company cannot significantly control or influence (the verb is printed "presecribed" —
as printed/extracted); and procedures for **corporate governance and oversight of the actuarial function**, with a
process for waiver or modification.

**¶24.d — the decisive one for non-PBR business (PDF p. 1197 [A820–12]).** For policies outside principle-based
valuation under ¶¶25–27, the minimum standard must take one of two forms. Option **i.**, quoted because the library
cites its exact words: "Be consistent with the minimum standard of valuation prior to the operative date of the
Valuation Manual". Option **ii.**: produce reserves that quantify the contracts' benefits, guarantees and funding,
and their risks, at a conservatism level reflecting conditions that include unfavourable events reasonably likely to
occur.

**¶24.e** — other requirements, the printed list being non-exhaustive: reserve methods; risk models and the generation
of economic scenarios; assumptions and margins; use of company experience; risk measurement; disclosure;
certifications and reports; actuarial opinions and memoranda; **transition rules**; and internal controls.

**¶24.f** — the data and form of data required under ¶28, to whom submitted, and possibly further analyses.

**And ¶27, one sentence (PDF p. 1198 [A820–13]):** a principle-based valuation may contain "a prescribed formulaic
reserve component".

**¶24.a.i–ii + ¶24.d.i + ¶27 together are the chain:** the Valuation Manual must still specify CRVM for life and CARVM
for annuities; for non-PBR business the standard may simply track the minimum standard in force before the operative
date; and a principle-based valuation is expressly permitted to contain a prescribed formulaic component. That is
A-820's own account of why a VM-20 net premium reserve routes back to formulaic requirements, and
why a CRVM engine is still required equipment in 2026.

### 11. Requirements of a principle-based valuation (A-820 ¶¶25–26, 28)

**¶25 — the four conditions a company must meet (PDF pp. 1197–1198 [A820–12/13]):**

- **a.** Quantify the contracts' benefits, guarantees and funding, and their risks, at a conservatism level reflecting
  conditions that include unfavourable events reasonably likely to occur **during the lifetime of the contracts**; and,
  for contracts with significant **tail risk**, reflect conditions adverse enough to quantify that tail risk. (The
  printed text spells "polices" for "policies" — as printed/extracted.)
- **b.** Use assumptions, risk-analysis methods, financial models and management techniques that track the company's
  overall risk assessment process — the test is "consistent with, but not necessarily identical to" — allowing for
  differences in financial-reporting structure and for any prescribed assumptions or methods.
- **c.** Take each assumption in one of two ways: **as prescribed in the Valuation Manual**; or, where nothing is
  prescribed, from the company's own available experience **to the extent it is relevant and statistically credible**,
  falling back on other relevant, statistically credible experience where company data is unavailable, irrelevant or
  not credible.
- **d.** Include margins for uncertainty — adverse deviation and estimation error among them — sized so that **more
  uncertainty means a larger margin and so a larger reserve**.

**¶26 — obligations of a company using PBR (PDF p. 1198 [A820–13]):** (a) set up **corporate governance and
oversight** procedures for the actuarial valuation function, consistent with the Valuation Manual; (b) give the
commissioner **and the board of directors** a yearly certification of how effective its internal controls over the
PBR valuation are — controls that **must be designed** to ensure that every material risk in the liabilities **and
associated assets** is captured and that valuations follow the Valuation Manual — the certification resting on the
controls in place at the end of the preceding calendar year; (c) prepare, and **file with the commissioner upon
request**, a PBR valuation report meeting the Valuation Manual's standards.

**¶28 — experience reporting (PDF p. 1198 [A820–13]):** a company must submit the mortality, morbidity,
policyholder-behaviour or expense experience, and other data, that the Valuation Manual prescribes.

### 12. A-821 — annuity mortality: purpose, formulas, worked example, tables

**Purpose (¶1, PDF p. 1199 [A821–1]).** To recognize four tables for the minimum standard of valuation for annuity
and pure endowment contracts: **1983 Table "a"**, the **Annuity 2000 Mortality Table**, the **2012 Individual
Annuity Reserving (2012 IAR) Table**, and the **1994 Group Annuity Reserving (1994 GAR) Table**.

**Definitions (¶¶2–9, PDF p. 1199 [A821–1]), the load-bearing ones:**

- **¶5 "Period table"** — a table of mortality rates for one given calendar year, that year being "the Period".
- **¶6 "Generational mortality table"** — a table whose rates for a given age decline from one year to the next,
  **built by combining a Period table with a projection scale of mortality-improvement rates**.
- **¶7 "2012 IAR Table"** — the **Generational** table developed by the SOA Committee on Life Insurance Research,
  whose rates q<sub>x</sub><sup>2012+n</sup> come from combining the 2012 IAM Period Table with Projection Scale G2 by
  the ¶¶13–14 method.
- **¶8 "2012 IAM Period Table"** (printed in full as the "2012 Individual Annuity Mortality Period Life (2012 IAM
  Period) Table") — the Period table of **loaded** mortality rates q<sub>x</sub><sup>2012</sup> for calendar year 2012,
  developed by the same SOA committee and printed as Appendices I and II.
- **¶9 "Projection Scale G2"** — the table of annual mortality-improvement rates G2<sub>x</sub> by age, used to
  project mortality beyond calendar year 2012; developed by the same committee and printed as Appendices III and IV.

**¶13 — application of the 2012 IAR Table (PDF p. 1200 [A821–2]),** in the library's symbols:

```
q_x^(2012+n) = q_x^2012 · (1 − G2_x)^n
```

**¶14 — the rounding rule and worked example (PDF p. 1200 [A821–2]).** Each q<sub>x</sub><sup>2012+n</sup> is
rounded to **three decimal places per 1,000** (the printed illustration is 0.741 deaths per 1,000), and the rounding
is applied to the ¶13 formula evaluated **from the 2012 Period-table rate** each time. The printed example is a male
aged 30, with q<sub>x</sub><sup>2012</sup> = 0.741 and a G2 rate of 0.010; in symbols:

```
q_x^2013 = 0.741 × (1 − 0.010)^1 = 0.73359    → rounded 0.734
q_x^2014 = 0.741 × (1 − 0.010)^2 = 0.7262541  → rounded 0.726
```

¶14 then shows the method it rules out — chaining from the already-rounded 2013 rate, 0.734 × 0.99 = 0.727 — and
states in terms that computing q<sub>x</sub><sup>2014</sup> from the rounded q<sub>x</sub><sup>2013</sup> is
incorrect.

(Cross-check against Appendix II and Appendix IV: male age 30 has 1000·q<sub>x</sub><sup>2012</sup> = 0.741 and
G2<sub>x</sub> = 0.010 — the example is internally consistent with the printed tables. These two values are kept
because `products/immediate_annuity/technical-notes.md` and `products/immediate_annuity/product-spec.md` cite them as
confirmed against the printed tables.)

**¶16 — application of the 1994 GAR Table (PDF p. 1200 [A821–2]),** in the library's symbols:

```
q_x^(1994+n) = q_x^1994 · (1 − AA_x)^n      with q_x^1994 and AA_x taken from the 1994 GAR Table itself
```

**Modelling consequence.** The 2012 IAR and 1994 GAR are **generational**: the valuation mortality rate is a function of
**(age, calendar year)**, and the rounding rule forbids the recursive `q(y+1) = q(y)·(1−G2)` shortcut. Implement as
`q(x, 2012+n) = round3( q2012[x] * (1 − G2[x])**n )` evaluated from the **2012 base every time**, with the year offset
*n* recomputed at each projection step. A single-vector `q[x]` array cannot represent this basis.

### 13. A-822 — asset adequacy analysis requirements (the whole appendix)

Four paragraphs, and the appendix contains nothing else (all on PDF p. 1205 [A822–1]).

- **¶1, definition:** "asset adequacy analysis" is an analysis of whether reserves and related actuarial items,
  viewed against the assets that support them, are adequate to meet the insurer's obligations.
- **¶2, the adequacy requirement:** the reserves and related items, viewed with the assets the company holds for them
  — the investment earnings on those assets and the considerations expected to be received and retained under the
  contracts both counted — must adequately provide, "according to presently accepted actuarial standards of
  practice", for the cash flows that the company's contractual obligations and related expenses are expected to
  require.
- **¶3, the additional reserve:** where the company concludes from asset adequacy analysis that it should hold a
  reserve beyond the aggregate reserve held and calculated under the methods of **Appendix A-820**, it "shall establish
  the additional reserve".
- **¶4, release:** additional reserves so established that later prove unnecessary may be released, and the release
  "would not be deemed an adoption of a lower standard of valuation".

**A-822 ¶3 is the AP&P locus of the "asset adequacy analysis is part of minimum reserves" rule**, and it is expressly
additive to the A-820 aggregate. **A-822 ¶4 read with A-820 ¶18** is the pair that keeps establishment and release of
the AAT additional reserve outside the change-in-valuation-basis accounting.

    "Issued before 1/1/2017 → formulaic CRVM remains the minimum standard, fixed by year of issue" — is confirmed

20. **A rounding detail an implementer will hit.** A-820 ¶7.a.i prescribes rounding "to the nearer one-quarter of one

21. **¶24.a.i–ii, ¶24.d.i and ¶27 are the citation the library wanted for its own sentence.** The claim that "whole

### What these three appendices do NOT address

  benefits must be valued, how partial withdrawals, free-partial provisions, bonus recapture, GLWB/GMWB streams or
  index credits enter. That is AG 33 / AG 35 territory and neither is in the extracted material.

  here.

  Select Mortality Factors, 1980 CSO, 1980 CSO with Ten-Year Select Mortality Factors, 1961 CSI, the 1952 Disability

### Ambiguities and internal oddities in the printed text

1. **¶12's "those paragraphs" / "that paragraph".** The plural/singular mismatch is a renumbering artefact from the
   model law's "Subsection A" / "Subsection A(1)". **A-820 as printed does not pin the 15% reduction to ¶11.a** — the
   reading that it reduces *α* comes from the model law, not from this text. Flag it wherever the library states it.
2. **¶20 refers to "paragraphs 19 and 20" from inside ¶20.** Artefact of the same renumbering ("Subsection A and this
   Subsection B"). Read as "¶19 and this ¶20".
3. **¶22's empty window.** Its second sentence addresses contracts issued from January 1, 2017 up to, but not
   including, the operative date, while ¶3/¶4 fix the operative date **at** January 1, 2017. As printed the window is
   empty.
4. **¶7's "effective date of the Codification"** is an applicability threshold expressed by reference to an event whose
   date A-820 never prints. **Do not supply a date for it from memory.**
5. **¶24.c.ii prints "presecribed"** for "prescribed"; **¶25.a prints "polices"** for "policies". Both recorded above
   as found; each is either a print error or an extraction error and cannot be told apart from the text layer.
6. **A-820's header omits Model #820** while listing #817 and #821 — noted above; A-822 is the appendix that names #820.
7. **¶1's definitions are limited to "on or after the operative date of the Valuation Manual"**, yet several of them
   (notably ¶1.e's inclusion of annuities within "life insurance") would, if read backwards into ¶¶5–22, conflict with
   that half's separate life/annuity tracks. Read ¶1 as scoped to ¶¶23–28.
8. **¶8.c.ii's increments are not monotone in plan type** (A +.15, B +.25, C +.05). This looks like a transcription
